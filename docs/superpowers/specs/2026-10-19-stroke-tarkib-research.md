## Thuluth stroke research (thread 1, 2026-10-19)

Labels: [A] means a source states it; [I] means inference.

### The main source

MEB, *Hüsn-i Hat Atölyesi 10* (in Şevki Efendi's style):
https://meslek.meb.gov.tr/upload/dersmateryali/pdf/H%C3%9CSN-%C4%B0%20HAT%20AT%C3%96LYES%C4%B0%2010%20(Geleneksel%20T%C3%BCrk%20Sanatlar%C4%B1).pdf

- Local copy: scratchpad\dl_meb\hat10.pdf.
- Rendered pages: scratchpad\meb_pages\.
- Each letter's page has direction arrows, numbered moves, dot measures and an exploded view.
- The key on p.71: the numbers give the order and the count of moves.
- Pages 95–114 are a 120-frame photo sequence of the letters being written.

### The pen

- Derman (Türk Maarif Ansiklopedisi, "Hat"):
  - each part of a letter is drawn without lifting the pen, following the grip's direction;
  - the whole nib edge is on the paper;
  - a top-to-bottom stroke comes out thin, a right-to-left one thick.
  - [I] This means a fixed nib angle within each part.
- The attested manipulation is lifting one corner of the nib (MEB pp.76, 79, 85):
  - ra tail: lift the ünsî side (the side nearest the writer) to end sharp;
  - ta nose: lift the ünsî side and finish on the vahşî side (the far side);
  - mim tail: lift the ünsî side.
- Holding angle varies by source:
  - Leeds: 60–70° for the alif, about 80° for the ba's opening stroke, then vertical for the bowl;
  - other blogs give 45°, 60° and 35°.
  - [I] The low values probably describe the slant of the nib's cut, not how it is held.
- The nuqta is measured on its diagonal (Derman). It is the pen's mark pulled one pen-width. Measures are given in whole and half dots, and "less than half" (MEB p.68).

### Proportions, in nuqta

- Alif: 7. It is built as the zülfe (head tuft), then 2 of chest, 2 of body and 2 of tail (TDV, quoted in MEB p.92).
- Ba (Leeds): bowl 6 wide inside, 1 deep. MEB's measure sheet shows about 6 across and 3 up.
- Kaf: bowl 5 inside. Lam: bowl 2 deep.
- Jeem (MEB p.74): belly as tall as the alif; the tail reaches further out than the head.
- Joins and gaps are measured in dots: the Rabbiyessir sheet (MEB p.56) gives 6, 3, 4, ½ and less than ½.

### Parts letters share (MEB)

- The kef's and lam's uprights are an alif.
- The fe's bowl (tekne) is the ba's.
- The qaf has the fe's head and a waw-like bowl.
- Alif, fa, kef and lam carry the zülfe.
- Letters are taught by group; sin can be written with teeth or as a kashida.
- Leeds: the kaf's and lam's horizontal is the ba's second stroke.

### Stroke order (MEB move counts)

Counts: alif 2, hamza 3, ba 3, jeem 4, dal 3, ra 3, sin 5, sad 4, ta 4, ayn 4, fa 3, qaf 3, kaf 3, lam 3, mim 3, nun 3, waw 3, ha 5, lam-alif 4, ya 3.

- Alif: the zülfe dot first, then the alif pulled out of it; the tip finished with the pen's corner.
- Ba: a short downstroke, the bowl going down and left, then the last third rising.
- Jeem: an opening mark, the head bar left to right, a sharp turn into the descent, then the bowl sweeping right into the tail. The photos suggest a pen lift after the opening mark [I]; the tail is filled from below with the corner if needed.
- Ayn: the eyebrow left to right, down the left side, the inner curve, then the tail.
- Mim: the head as a loop, then the tail thinned by lifting the ünsî side.
- Ya: head, turn, then the return bowl.
- Lam-alif: lam head, the diagonal, the base loop, then the alif arm (its arrow appears to point upward [I]).
- Sin: 5 moves; the part after the second tooth sits lower.
- Ra and dal: the head pulled out of the zülfe.
- ـيا was not found.

### Joins and tarkib

- Unsettled: no source says whether a connection continues the previous letter's stroke or where the pen lifts inside a word.
- Joins are measured in dots (Rabbiyessir).
- Joins are taught after isolated letters, then istif (stacked layouts).
- Sherif & Fahmy: the kashida is shared between the two letters.
- Zakariya hides restarts by trimming with a knife.
- Nothing found on how stacked letters connect.

### Tashkil

Al-Maghribi, https://www.cdf.gov.eg/khate/?q=node/2237:
- Pens: ⅓ of the writing pen, ½ of it, and the writing pen itself.
- Fatha:
  - short 3–4, medium 4–6, long 9–11 (long uses the writing pen too);
  - all fathas must be parallel;
  - a long fatha only where there is an a-vowel;
  - it may bend, except a full-pen fatha;
  - tanwin is two parallel strokes, never bent.
- Kasra: short or medium only, ⅓ pen, never bent.
- Damma: either pen.
- Sukun: two forms.
- Shadda: tashkil pen.
- Order of writing: text, dots, harakat, ornaments.
- Small reading-aid letters: ح س ص ط ع ك م go under the letter; ك and ه go above.

MEB p.91 chart of shapes:
- madda: a long, slightly curved stroke;
- dagger alif;
- sukun: a ring or a jeem head;
- shadda: w-shaped;
- damma: a small waw with a tail;
- fatha and kasra: diagonals rising to the left;
- ornaments: tırnak, tirfil and mimli tirfil, placed to balance the ink.

## Prior art: Arabic calligraphy as pen strokes (research thread 2, 2026-10-19)

Labels: [A] read in the primary source; [S] secondary; [I] inference.
1 nuqta = 1.4 × pen width (AlQalam) ≈ 67 app units at 48 units per pen.

### AlQalam (Sherif & Fahmy)

Sources:
- https://tug.org/TUGboat/tb29-1/tb91sherif.pdf
- https://tug.org/TUGboat/tb29-3/tb93sherif.pdf
- https://tug.org/TUGboat/tb33-3/tb105mansour.pdf
- Thesis: http://www.eece.cu.edu.eg/~hfahmy/thesis/2008_01_paf.pdf

Strokes [A]:
- A stroke is a continuous pen movement, its points numbered in writing order and dependent on each other.
- Each is drawn with `qstroke(path, angle_start, angle_end)`, the nib angle interpolated along the path.
- Naskh nib angle is about 70°; the alif inside lam or kaf is drawn at 79°.
- 28 letters reduce to 17 skeletons, built from three kinds of primitive:
  - fixed: nuqta, hamza, shāma, the tail of rā/wāw;
  - slightly parametric: wāw head, alif;
  - very flexible: the kasa (3 to 13 nuqtas) and the kashida.
- The nūn bowl turns the nib from 70° to 120°.

Joins [A]:
- The kashida is a connection between both letters, not part of either.
- One shared `tatwil` parameter goes to both letters.
- They meet where the pen moves exactly horizontally: a direction constraint, not an outline splice.

Rendering [A]:
- `penstroke` (two rails) fails three ways: zero-width tips, figure-8 crossings, and big rotations.
- `qstroke` (stamping nib footprints along the path) was chosen.
- Tails (shaẓiya) are outlined with the nib corner and filled, not swept.

Hard parts [A]:
- Dots and diacritics, bad joins (ʿayn, qāf–ṣād, lām–dāl), cost.
- No Thuluth model and no public code.

### DecoType / Milo

Sources:
- https://www.tradigital.de/stories/studies/case-study-right-to-left-font-structure.pdf
- https://www.tradigital.de/stories/studies/case-study-technical-and-aesthetic-challenges.pdf

Findings:
- The unit of writing is the letter compound [A].
- Compounds sit on a secondary baseline of about 5° in Naskh [A].
- Layers: skeleton, then dots, then shadda, then vowels; attachments never alter the skeleton [A].
- Shaping balances assimilation against dissimilation [A].
- "The underlying calligraphic mechanism operates on the level of pen strokes regardless the graphemic status of the larger unit they build." [A]
- Ruqʿah was reduced to about 70 strokes plus rules [S].
- The stroke inventory and rules are proprietary [A].

### Lazrek / Benatia / Elyaakoubi; Bayar & Sami

Sources:
- Benatia: https://tug.org/TUGboat/tb27-2/tb87benatia.pdf
- Bayar & Sami: https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~dberry/ATEP/Slides/Keshide/HowFontCanRespectArabicCalligraphy.pdf

Benatia [A]:
- Naskh only; Thuluth is called difficult and excluded.
- Its kashida rules: allowed positions, one stretch per word, sīn + 2 nuqtas, and allographs that change with a kashida.

Bayar & Sami [A]:
- The nib is a rectangle; its corner follows a Bézier path, and the four edge sweeps are unioned.
- Exact for a fixed angle on each segment, split where the path runs parallel to the nib.
- The kashida drops in proportion to its length (12 nuqtas wide drops ½ nuqta).
- Some parts are drawn with the corner and filled.

### Thuluth and machine learning

- No skeleton-plus-nib work exists for Thuluth.
- Calliar (MIT licence; https://github.com/ARBML/Calliar):
  - 2,500 samples and 45k strokes, Thuluth included;
  - centreline trajectories, no nib.
- Yaghan 2020, PLOS ONE: theory only, how alif proportions follow from nib width and angle.
- DigitalKhatt (AGPL): MetaPost Naskh built from outlines, not pens.

### Envelope geometry

- Fixed angle: the stroke is the Minkowski sum of the path and a segment. The rails are P ± (L/2)u, and they swap where the path's tangent runs parallel to the nib.
- Rotating nib [I]: an interior point of the nib joins the envelope where s = −(P′·n)/θ′ lies within ±L/2. This is the figure-8 and missing-chunk case, and Thuluth's bowls and heads will hit it.
- Cusps and correctness: Levien & Uguray 2024 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00127).
- Tools:
  - MetaType1 `pen_stroke` (a nib for each node), used by Nupuram (OFL);
  - FontForge Expand Stroke (fixed angle; Remove Overlap may fail).
- No library does an exact envelope for a continuously rotating rectangular nib.
- Fallback [I]: stamp the nib as hulls, union them (Clipper2), then fit curves.

### Converting outlines into nib strokes

- No tool or paper does this.
- StrokeStyles (Berio et al. 2022): decomposition by medial axis; no code found.
- Route [I]:
  - width across direction φ = L·|sin(φ−θ)|, so the hairlines give θ and the thickest parts give L;
  - points across the stroke are separated by the nib vector, so the medial axis is not the pen path.

### Assessment (thread's)

- Port AlQalam's model and extend it:
  - each stroke is a centreline in nuqtas, with a nib angle at each node and a constant nib length;
  - the joins are shared strokes, each letter's end set by a direction constraint;
  - heads and tails get a second, "outlined and filled" primitive;
  - Milo's layering on top, and Benatia's rules for kashida.
- Risks, in order:
  1. robust envelopes in the browser;
  2. Thuluth rotates the nib far more than Naskh;
  3. no Thuluth nib data exists;
  4. converting the traced library is unproven;
  5. the best prior art is proprietary.

## The user's books (research thread 3, 2026-10-19)

Research only: the app never credits any author.

### The scans

- "Full" is the 2nd-edition rules book (78 pages). "Dha" is al-Dhahabi (39 pages). The Thuluth PDF is the same plates at lower resolution: its page = Full page − 4.
- Crops are in scratchpad\books\: f{N}_R and f{N}_L are the right and left halves of Full page N; d{N} are Dhahabi pages; z* are zooms.

### Pen technique (Full p7, prose)

- The alif's head is retouched with a fine pen after the stroke.
- The ain starts with a crescent drawn with the pen's corner, which is then filled.
- The heads of fa, qaf and waw are one shape, written with the full width of the pen; the roundness is finished with the pen's tip or a fine pen.
- Final connected ha: at the point marked ×, lift a third of the pen and continue, so the last part is thinner and higher.
- Mim: tilt the pen so it writes with a third of its width at the start, then return to the full width.

### Proportions in nuqta

- Alif height by how dense the composition is (Full p12):
  - plain line: 5–6
  - medium: 6–7
  - light: 7–8
  - heavy tarkib: 9–12
- The standalone alif ladder is about 7 (Full p9).
- The tall letters differ by 2 for lam, dal and final kaf, and by 1–1.5 for the rest (Full p22).
- Ha before a madd: 6 (Full p17).
- Waw mursal: 4, sitting in the cup of a nun or sin (Full p21).
- Ba: a connected ba of 1.5, and one of 3 connected to the last letter (Full p23).
- Dha p4 has nuqta ladders for the whole alphabet.

### Letters built from other letters' parts (the strongest material)

Full p21:
- fa, qaf and waw share one head;
- fa majmu' comes from ba;
- qaf majmu' from the cup of sin, sad and nun;
- waw mursal from ra mursal;
- waw majmu' from ra majmu' and dal;
- fa mursal from ba and the base of the zinadi kaf;
- qaf mursal from nun mursal.

Full p22, kaf:
- standalone: an alif plus the fa/ba base;
- final: the medial lam's shaft plus that base;
- zinadi: the head from a slanted ra, the first line from a low-headed ba, the second from a raised-headed ba;
- sayfi: from the start of lam-alif, with the head of a mursal sin;
- some derive it from dal.

Full p26, ha:
- the initial "cat's eye" ha: the head of dal plus a medial fa;
- the "horse's ear" ha: from the end of nun and the cups before it.

Other plates:
- Full p20: ain in three types (sha'bani, finjani, sadi), with arrows 1–6 and a construction triangle.
- Full p24–25: which mim form goes with which neighbours; mim-ra; the rahmani form.

### Stroke order

- Numbered arrows on Full p9, p20, p22, p24 and p27. Dha p2 has direction arrows for the whole alphabet.
- Full p16 and p22: the arrow numbers run on across connected letters (بنك, كتب) up to 8–11, which reads as one continuous sequence of moves through the joins [inference].
- Ghost dotted pen paths on p22, p24 and p26.

### Tashkil (Full p27)

- Each mark is drawn with numbered strokes: fatha, fathatan, damma, small ha, small mim, small ain, small jim/kha, small لله, small sin/sad/ta, small alif, small hamza, lam-alif, and a V-shaped ornament.
- Full p28 is the whole alphabet with vowels: fatha and kasra lie at the same slant as the letters' slanted strokes.
- No sizes in nuqta are given.
