# Jali Diwani: research notes (2026-10-19)

The first research for the Jali Diwani branch, done before any reference was
downloaded. Everything here was read from web pages; no book, PDF or image was
opened. Research only: the app credits no calligrapher. Book scans and crops
stay local and are never committed.

Labels: **[A]** a source states it (source given); **[I]** our inference.
Four threads: the pen, references to get, composition and fillers, and how a
meşk sheet teaches strokes.

## The pen

**Angle: the sources disagree on the number, and agree on the result.**
- 60°, "creating thin stems (verticals) and thick baselines (horizontals)":
  Kristyan Sarkis, from lessons with Ali Assi. [A]
  https://nuqta.com/kb/arabic-calligraphy-type-design (also
  https://tptq-arabic.com/articles/arabic_calligraphy_and_type_design)
- About 75°, "steeper … compared to other Arabic scripts". [A]
  https://uacalligraphy.ac/blog/diwani-6/majesty-of-diwani-episode-2-demystifying-the-fundamentals-of-diwani-15
- Close to 90° to the line (Khalil al-Kofahi, for scripts in general). [A]
  https://hibastudio.com/easy-calligraphy/
- Everyone agrees uprights come out thin and horizontals thick. [A]
- [I] The numbers likely measure different things (nib edge to line, shaft to
  paper). The nib is held more upright than for Thuluth. **Measure it from the
  references; don't take a number from these.**

**Cut (qatt).** No Diwani-specific measure.
- The dîvânî nib is cut "biraz düzce", a little flatter than the sülüs one. [A]
  https://hatsanat.wordpress.com/2009/05/10/hsn-hat-egitimi/ (anonymous blog)
- Derman ranks the slant of the cut, most to least: sülüs, ta'lik, nesih,
  rik'a. [A] https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kalem
- Diwani grew out of ta'lik. [A] https://kureansiklopedi.com/en/detay/divani-2
  [I] So a ta'lik-like cut, flatter than Thuluth's.

**Hairlines: three accounts, and they matter for how we draw.**
- **One pen, turned.** A skilled writer uses one pen and turns it for the
  letters with thin endings (a descending alif, lam, kaf, ha, mim). [A]
  Maasoum Khalaf, https://hibastudio.com/aldiwani/. Sarkis: "the thinnest
  strokes involve a lot of rotation and usage of the pen tip." [A]
- **A second, finer pen.** Letters are written with the full pen, then the
  fine tails of ا ج د و ر are finished with a finer one. [A]
  https://hyatok.com/الخط_الديواني:_أنواعه_وأهم_قواعده. For Jali the second pen
  is a quarter of the first's width. [A] Khalaf.
- These pages share wording; count them as one family of texts. [I]
- Ta'lik's hairlines are made with half the nib on the paper (Derman). [A]
  Diwani may inherit it. [I]
- [I] This is the Thuluth "fill" question again, in a sharper form: some tails
  may be finished with a fine pen, not swept by the nib. **Ask the user, and
  read the strokes off the references, before treating any tail as a nib
  stroke.**

**Jali is not simply Diwani with a larger pen.** It is written with a wider pen
than dîvânî, but it breaks the rule that a celî is three times the practice
pen. [A] Ali Alparslan, https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/celi. Diwani nib
up to 2 mm, Jali 3–5 mm. [A] Kofahi.

## Proportions

- **Alif:** 5 dots with a nib of 2 mm or less [A] (Kofahi); "stem height is 6
  rhombic dots" [A] (Sarkis); a 4 + 3 dot guide [A] (hyatok, low confidence,
  perhaps borrowed from Ruq'a). In celî dîvânî the alif and lam are lengthened
  on purpose. [A, search summary only]
- In Jali the two guide lines are one alif apart. [A] Kofahi, Khalaf.
- **Lengths of single moves, in dots,** in three schools: Ottoman (Mehmed
  İzzet) / Egyptian (Mustafa Ghazlan) / Baghdadi (Hashim). [A] Thaer
  al-Atrakji and Abdullah bin Sawad, https://hibastudio.com/diwany-differance/.
  The dot is never defined, and these are moves, not heights. [I]

  | letter | moves | dots per move (Ottoman / Egyptian / Baghdadi) |
  |---|---|---|
  | alif mursal | 3 | 4/4/3 · 3/3.5/3 · 3/2/3 |
  | alif kasi (cup) | 3 | middle move 3/3/2.5 |
  | ha mursal | 4 | 1/1/1 · 2/2/2 · 3/4/3 · 2/5/4 |
  | dal | 3 | 2/2/2 · 2/2/2 · 2/2.5/2 |
  | ra | 1 | 3/5/4 |
  | medial ba | 1 | 3/4/3 |

  Also: the ba drops 2 dots below the line in all three; the Egyptian alif
  2.5; the Ottoman sin's teeth rise 2 dots over a 3-dot cup; the Ottoman style
  is the small one, the Egyptian twice as tall. [A]
- [I] Diwani has no strict measure system like the six scripts; the Jali is
  said to follow "dot rules like Thuluth" [A] (Khalaf). The studio and the
  scans will have to give the real numbers, in pens.

## Letters, moves and joins

- **Bowls are drawn back from left to right:** ح س ص ع ق ك ل م ن ى. [A] TDV
  "Divanî", Ali Alparslan, https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/divani
- **ا د ر و join the letter after them** in Diwani, though they never do in
  the other scripts. [A] TDV; https://calligraphyqalam.com/styles/divani/
  [I] The layout's joining rules can't be borrowed from Thuluth's.
- Letters and words are written with almost no gap. [A] TDV.
- Uprights end in a slight curve at the bottom. [A] UAC (above).
- Jali shares plain Diwani's letter forms and joins, except the ع/ح group. [A]
  Khalaf; https://sana.sy/culture-and-arts/2554382/
- **No written source gives stroke direction or pen lifts for any letter.**
  The move counts above are the only ones found. Moves are taught by showing
  (below), so the videos and the studio's own stroke split are the sources.

## Composition and fillers

- **The line rises to the left**, ending in a sharp point; uprights lean left;
  horizontals slope from right to left. [A] TDV "Divanî". No source gives an
  angle. [I] Measure it from panels.
- Celî dîvânî is written in "a channel-shaped line whose end rises upward". [A]
  https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/istif. It is bounded top and bottom [A]
  (Khalaf; sotor.com; learnarabicuae.com) and is mostly ship-shaped, sometimes
  a circle [A] (Khalaf; Abdelnasser Al-Masri in SANA). [I] A ship is a band
  with straight top and bottom and closed ends; the accounts fit together.
- **Letters cut across each other** as in Thuluth and interlock both ways. [A]
  TDV "Celî"; learnarabicuae. No source says which pass under which.
- The calligrapher may change the length, size and place of letters to fit;
  alifs are lengthened; alif and jim need the most retouching. [A] SANA;
  kenanaonline (a blog).
- **Fillers.** Gaps are filled with "decorative marks and small dots", and
  leaving no gap is the rule. [A] TDV "Celî". Jali uses the six scripts'
  vowel marks (üstün, esre, ötre, şedde, cezim, hemze, tenvin, med, sıla) plus
  tirfil, tırnak, the marks on undotted letters, and "small dots", the last
  unique to celî dîvânî. [A] Kahya thesis abstract,
  https://tezara.org/theses/994953
- **The dots are round,** not rhombic. [A] kenanaonline; SANA. [I] One touch
  of a broad nib makes a rhombus, so a round dot is drawn and filled, or made
  by turning the pen. **Ask the user.**
- **The marks' pen:** for Jali Thuluth they use a separate pen a third to a
  quarter as wide; only the fatha (sometimes others) with the main pen, to
  close a gap. [A] TDV "İstif". For Jali Diwani this is [I].
- Plain Diwani has no ornament and no vowel marks. [A] TDV "Divanî"; Sarkis.
- Large jali work is planned on a pricked template and dusted onto the surface.
  [A] TDV "Celî".

## How a meşk teaches strokes

- The order is müfredat (single letters, then each letter joined with every
  other) and then mürekkebat (words and lines). [A] TDV "Meşk", Muhittin
  Serin, https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/mesk; Uğur Derman,
  https://www.fikriyat.com/yazarlar/ugur-derman/2017/08/29/hat-sanati-11-bir-hattatin-yetismesi
- Dîvânî has no "Rabbi yessir" opening; it starts with the single letters and
  their starting and finishing forms. [A, abstract] Betül Sayın 2026, TEKE,
  https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/teke/article/1785543
- The teacher corrects by rewriting under the student's line, measuring with
  dots and describing "kalemin akışı", the pen's flow. The method is shown,
  not written down. [A] Derman; TDV.
- **No written convention for reading stroke direction off a sheet was
  found.** [I] It can be read from the ink: a stroke is thickest where it runs
  across the nib; a gradual change of width means the pen turned; a hairline
  end means the edge or a lifted corner; where strokes cross, the later lies on
  top. That is the method already used for Thuluth (`stroke-ledger.md`).
- The printed model still taught from is **Halim Özyazıcı's** meşk (written
  from 1958 for Ali Alparslan), alongside Mehmed İzzet's. [A] Sayın;
  https://shop.ircica.org/shop/2075

## Decisions

- **2026-10-20, the user: where the studio and a master's plate disagree,
  the master's plate wins.** The studio is the starting outline and the
  piece split; the plates judge the shape and the stroke order.

## What this changes from Thuluth [I]

1. The nib is held steeper; Thuluth's 145–149° and its 1.65 pens to the nuqta
   do not carry over. Both are measured afresh.
2. Fine tails may be a second pen's work, and the round filler dots may be
   drawn and filled. Both are fill questions for the user.
3. ا د ر و join forward, and bowls come back left to right: the joining and
   the stroke directions are this script's own.
4. Lines climb to the left and fit a ship-shaped band: a composition matter
   for later.
