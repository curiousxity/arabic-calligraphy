("watch these and record the steps"). They were written from the lessons' spoken

## What matters for fitting pen strokes

Gathered from all 34 lessons. Each point cites the lesson and time where it is taught.

- **The nib angle is not fixed.** The basic hold is 70–75°. The nib turns more upright for
  width, as on the left side of the Ha body (L4 [8:43], [12:19]) and the start of the Dal's
  back (L7 [2:06]). It is very flat at the start of Sad and Ta, then steepens on the way down
  (L11 [11:28], L12 [1:02]–[1:32]). Dots are made at about 60°, 1¼ pen widths long.
- **Thin ends come from turning the nib or finishing on its corner.** The pen is not lifted
  mid-stroke. Examples are the Ra tails (L8), the end of the Ha body (L4 [9:47]) and the Alif's
  foot (L1 [20:33]). The final Ha's hook goes from the full nib to two thirds, then to the tip
  (L28 [3:16]).
- **Some shapes are two passes over each other.** The tall middle tooth of Ba/Ta/Ya goes up
  curving left, then straight down, and the two passes don't fully overlap. That is why the tooth
  is thin at the top and thick in the middle (L3 [5:11]–[6:47]). The connected Dal's back is
  written the same way (L7 [8:48]).
- **Heads are written first, and some are finished with the tip.** The Alif's head ornament is
  started, then the shaft is written, then the ornament is finished (L1 [10:41]–[15:18]). The
  head of Fa, Qaf and Waw is two moves: up and to the left, then the pen turns and comes back.
  It stops square and is never dragged back, then is rounded with the pen's tip (L15 [1:32],
  [3:08]; L17 [4:13]–[5:47]). The Ain's head is partly a stroke and partly filled with the tip
  (L13 [0:31]–[1:34]). A single fitted stroke cannot match those outlines.
- **Seen teeth are separate moves that stop.** Each curves up, then down, and the low points
  between teeth step lower one after another (L9, L10).
- **Pen size.** The teacher changes to a smaller pen in several places: the 12-nuqta long Seen
  (L9 [6:39], L10 [4:04]), the big gathered Ain (L13 [6:11]), the Kaf's cap (L18 [7:41]), and
  words (L11 [8:45], L19 [9:51], L21 [6:16]). He says it is to fit the board, so it is not taken
  as part of the letter; whether the long Seen is different is open. Diacritics are a different
  case: they *are* written with a pen a third of the letters' width, except the long fatha that
  fills a gap, one sukun form, and fillers in a big space (L31).
- **Meem** (L22–24): the head is written in moves. Move 1 rises, turns over and comes down the
  right side, which is nearly straight. Move 2 starts just behind it, leaving a small knot, and
  closes the loop. Move 3 is the neck and tail. Whether the pen lifts between head moves is never
  said. The counter should look like a cone.
- **Ya** (L29): the head is a dot-like move (up to the corner, stop, turn, come back). Then comes
  a down-curve about 2 nuqta long that drops about 2.5, then an S-curve (a sag and a crest joined
  fairly sharply). The tail is a Noon move, 2 nuqta high and 5 wide, started with "take the pen,
  put it here".
- **Composing** (L33–34): the order is a pencil baseline, a sketch at the pen's real scale,
  letters, dots, harakat, then fillers. Space is filled by choosing letter variants (Meem, Ha,
  Ra, Kaf, Noon), not marks. Keep 1–2 nuqta between letters, and never let two thick turns land
  on the same spot. Neighbouring words touch end to start. All alifs and Lam-Alifs reach the same
  height. "Allah" goes top centre, then top right, then top left, never low. Words are never
  reordered to fit.

## Lesson 01: Alef (pen, hold, scaling nuqta, letter dots; isolated Alef with helia; Alef connected from the right)

Terms used in all notes: **nuqta** = the scaling unit (the teacher says "points" or "dots"). **Letter dot** = an i'jam dot written on a letter (not a measurement). **Helia** (hilya) = the small head ornament on top of Alef, Dal, Ra and some Ba'a teeth.

### Pen, hold, and the three kinds of dot [1:01]
1. Use a bamboo pen. Glossy paper.
2. Cut: the nib angle is much steeper than for Ruq'a. Take the flat side of the pen; the cut runs down to a point slightly short of half its width ("about one millimetre", then two halves next to each other). People quote 45 degrees or "40-something"; the teacher says use the pen itself as the guide.
3. This cut is for Thuluth only. It will not work for Farsi, Ruq'a or Diwani. Do not use a Ruq'a-cut pen for Thuluth.
4. Hold the pen a couple of centimetres back from the tip. Hold it lightly, not stressed.
5. Hold the cut at about 70-75 degrees. Start at 75. The angle changes during writing; do not be fussed about it.

Scaling nuqta (two kinds) [3:34]:
- **Vertical scaling nuqta**: pen at about 75 degrees, move right and downward. Slightly diamond shaped, a bit stretched. Use it to measure heights.
- **Horizontal scaling nuqta**: turn the pen to a more vertical angle and move to the right. It looks fatter. Use it to measure widths.
- The two have different angles; do not mix them.

Letter dots (i'jam) [5:04]:
1. Hold the pen at about 60 degrees.
2. Move down to the right, perpendicular to that angle.
3. Make it long: one pen width plus a quarter. It should feel "really far".
- Two dots: side by side, and they overlap slightly (the left dot's end overlaps the right dot's start). Or one on top of the other.
- Three dots (Sheen, Tha'): either a triangle or three side by side. The third dot sits in a smaller space and sticks out; do not try to fit it inside the edges of the other two. Stacked vertically is very rare; stick to triangle or side by side.

Warm-up [7:05]: hold the pen at the stated angle and pull straight lines down by moving the whole arm.

### Isolated Alef [7:36]
Two parts: the helia on top, then the Alef shaft.

Measurements:
- Height: 7 nuqta standard (vertical nuqta). General range 7 to 12.
- In compositions (tarakib, Jali) Alef can be short: down to 5, and 4 nuqta when used with Ba'a [9:06].
- Helia: 1.5 nuqta high, 1 nuqta wide.
- Offset: mark half a nuqta to the left of the scale column, so the helia does not overlap to the right. Then 1 nuqta for the helia width. This gives a small rectangle for the head [10:10].
- The bottom curve of the Alef is 1.5 nuqta high [15:18].
- Measured straight down, the foot ends half a nuqta from the start line (because the start was offset half a nuqta) [14:47].

Steps:
1. Helia, first pass [10:41]: in the rectangle, measure half a nuqta. Place the pen so the cut touches the top line and the left intersection (the half-nuqta mark).
2. Move downward, aimed towards the lower edge of the helia box. Stop partway. Do not finish the helia yet.
3. Divide the helia's top into thirds [13:16]. Third 1 sits over the Alef shaft; the shaft leaves from it. Third 2 is inside the Alef. Third 3 is the decoration that sticks out.
4. Shaft, part 1 [11:42]: go down with a slight curve to the right.
5. Part 2: a straight line, no curve. It may be thicker.
6. Part 3: a slight curve back to the left (1.5 nuqta high). Then go straight, go left, and stop.
7. At the very end, as you move to the right, you can lift the pen a little and finish on the tip of the nib so the end is neat [20:33].
8. Go back to the top and finish the helia [15:18].

Helia details (show only with a big pen) [15:49]:
- Its top edge has three curves: third 1 curves one way (down), third 2 curves back up, third 3 goes down and curves back to finish.
- The inner corner where helia meets shaft is not sharp with a big pen: there is a small step ("up, right, down, left") left by carving the shaft [16:50].
- The right edge of the helia is not written with the pen. Connect the top edge down to the shaft edge; there is a small chamfer where they meet. Some people do this with paper [17:50].
- Key points: keep the scale, do not let the helia end too far to the right, and know which third the shaft leaves from [19:22].

Warnings:
- The exaggerated curves on the board are for teaching; real curves are slight [12:15].
- Foot end differs between calligraphers (thick, thin, small nudge). Keep it simple: go straight, go left, stop. Do not overthink it [14:17].

Unclear: [11:12] "move towards this edge down here" (which corner the first helia pass aims at); [17:20] exact shape of the inner corner step; [18:21] the right-edge chamfer and what joins to what; [3:03] the 70 vs 75 degree hold shown by hand.

### Alef connected from the right (final Alef) [21:03]
Measurements: 7 nuqta high (governed by the Alef height). Ends half a nuqta to the left, like the isolated Alef.

Steps:
1. Come up from the connection with a smooth curve.
2. A straight line, inclined to the left.
3. The last part sticks out further to the left.
- Three parts: curve up, straight, out to the left.

Notes:
- Thickness changes near the top as it goes left: it "opens slightly", "blossoming". These details make the script look alive [22:04].

Unclear: [22:04] where exactly the thickness change happens ("it's thin here and then it changes").

## Lesson 02: Ba'a (isolated Ba'a, open-start variant, Ba'a + Alef, how to practise)

General notes [0:00]:
- Think of every move in three parts ("thirds"), not as one move. For Ba'a: go down; then adjust the direction; then go into the curve.
- Initial Ba'a has 10 shapes, each chosen by the letter that follows. Write down which shape goes with which case [1:02].
- Pen cleaning: wipe the pen with water and tissue after every session; hand sanitiser also works and evaporates (skip for stroke work).

### Isolated Ba'a [2:36]
Measurements:
- First move (the tooth): 1.5 nuqta high, with a lean of about half a nuqta "or a quarter of a nuqta" (teacher gives both).
- 1 nuqta under the pen's start: how high the letter sits above the writing line [4:07].
- Width inside the letter: 6 nuqta (horizontal nuqta).
- Under the third of those six nuqta, one more nuqta: it governs the drop [4:38].
- The descending run: 3 nuqta.
- The tail ends half a nuqta below the top line [6:39].

Steps:
1. Tooth [3:07]: hold the pen at the usual angle, start at the top, move downward with a curve. Result: a tusk shape, like the Arabic numeral 1.
2. Body, part 1 [4:38]: from the foot of the tooth, move left, pointing downward, for 3 nuqta. Use the 6-nuqta row and the drop nuqta to check you go down enough.
3. Body, part 2 [5:39]: adjust the downward move with a slight curve. It is not a curve upward yet. The teacher calls this a very important move; it gives the full shape.
4. Body, part 3 [6:09]: a circular move that joins the scale points. Keep moving up along the circle and stop half a nuqta below the top line.
- The joint between tooth and body: angular on the inside, curved on the outside (like the connected Alef). You may need to go back and fix that angle [3:37], [5:09].
- The curve is large and round, different from other scripts. Give it a "massive circular shape".
- Stroke weight: the first part is thinner than the second because the pen meets it at a shallow angle; the second is thick. The curve comes from turning the pen [22:57].

Warnings:
- Letter sitting below the writing line is wrong [24:28].
- An end that is too straight, or a bad end curve, is wrong; redo it [24:28].
- A body that is too thick: it should be thinner [19:04].

Unclear: [4:07] whether the 1-nuqta mark is above or below the writing line ("below this pen you will need one point"); [7:09] "after these four points I'm going to stop, think and go back and do the circular move" (four points vs three?); [3:37] the inside vs outside joint shape shown on the board.

### Isolated Ba'a, open-start variant [7:09]
The start is the move that also begins Kaf. Same height as before (the 1-nuqta mark).

Steps:
1. Place the pen, move up a little, then move down [7:41].
2. Add the 6-nuqta width row. Same curvature rules as the normal Ba'a. 1 nuqta below governs the drop.
3. Move down for 3 nuqta (curving up, then curving down).
4. Flatten it out.
5. Final curve: more to the left than the normal one [8:42].
6. Lower part [9:14]: under the 1-nuqta starting point add another nuqta; the shape drops to it. Inside, it is the first upward curve plus a second curve that joins back.
7. The thin wing [9:44]: change pen, or keep the same pen and change the angle you hold it at to get a thin line. Place the pen, move slightly down, then curve backwards. This gives a sharp start and then a smooth curve, a "wing" shape.
- If you continue this shape downward you get the top of Kaf (needed later in the Kaf lesson) [10:45].

Unclear: [10:45] "the cap for the letter calvin the lift" (Kaf and Alef? (?)); [9:14] exactly where the lower part ends.

### Ba'a connected to Alef (Ba'a-Alef) [11:15]
Measurements: tooth 1.5 nuqta high; 1 nuqta of drop; 1 nuqta between the two letters; the Alef goes out half a nuqta and up 7 nuqta (or whatever Alef height you use).

Steps:
1. Tooth: 1.5 nuqta, with its curve.
2. Move left with the same 1-nuqta drop.
3. Curve and turn up into the Alef, continuing upward.
4. Alef: like the connected Alef in lesson 01.
- Inside angular, outside curvier, as before.
- The two must not be too close or too far apart.

### Long Ba'a (mentioned only) [25:28]
- There is a 12-nuqta long ending used for Ba'a and Noon to add elongations. Taught later.
- Big initial Ba'a shapes depend on what follows (one used with Ha' and Meem, one with Ha' then Ya'). Taught later.

### Practice method [14:49]
- Rule lines. Put the scale before each letter.
- To sit the letter on the line: measure the top part (about 3 nuqta), then the 1.5 and the 1, so the letter lands on the line instead of dropping below it [16:19].
- Re-add the 6-nuqta width scale and keep going. Fill the page.
- Focus on the moves being right, not on how the ink behaves [19:51].
- Review each line: cross out letters below the line, with bad ends, or too straight.
- Practice words: Ab (Alef 7 nuqta + Ba'a), Bab. The letter dot of Ba'a goes below.

Unclear: [20:21] where the letter dot of Ba'a sits ("down here", "this should be a bit more up").

## Lesson 03: Ba'a continued (Ba'a connected to Ba'a; runs of three and four teeth; elongated Ba'a inside a word)

Key idea [11:23]: count letters correctly. A tooth (nabra (?), the teacher's "nebula") plus its short run is the letter; the stroke between teeth is the connection. Letter, connection, letter, connection. This matters for placing letter dots.

### Recap: initial Ba'a before Alef [0:00]
- Tooth 1.5 nuqta high. Go down to the right, then left, leave 1 nuqta between the two letters, come back up into the Alef.
- Lean to the right, the drop, and a curved outside, as in lesson 02.

### Two Ba'as: initial Ba'a + final Ba'a [1:02]
Measurements: initial tooth 1.5 nuqta; 1 nuqta between the letters; the second (final) Ba'a starts half a nuqta below the first move; 6-nuqta width row; 1 nuqta below as the drop guide [2:04].

Steps:
1. Initial tooth: same as before, down to the right, curve, come back.
2. Stop half a nuqta below the first move. Reason: an isolated Ba'a sits 1 nuqta higher, but here the final Ba'a has no tooth of its own, so it drops below the first move [1:34].
3. Final Ba'a: curve up a little, then go down for 3 nuqta, then move into the end curve, as in lesson 02 [2:35].
- To control the end curve, add a mark about half a pen-cut wide (not half a nuqta) that shows where the two curves meet as tangents [3:05].

Unclear: [3:05] the tangent mark; what it is placed against.

### Three in a row: short, tall, short (e.g. Bayt) [3:35]
Rule [16:31]: with three or more teeth, the first is short, the second is tall, the third is short.

Measurements:
- First tooth: short. The teacher says "start with two points of height" at [3:35], but at [8:51] says the first is "one point high" and its lower part is 1 nuqta thick. (Conflict; check the board.)
- 1 nuqta between teeth.
- Middle tooth: 3 nuqta high, about 3.5 counting the half-nuqta step; 2 nuqta taller than the other two [4:38], [8:51].
- Half a nuqta of distance below at the break [9:21].

Steps:
1. First tooth: short. Go straight into the curve [4:07].
2. Middle tooth, up-stroke [4:38]: go up like an Alef, but curve to the left early, earlier than an Alef.
3. Middle tooth, down-stroke [5:11]: come back down in a straight move, not along the same curve.
- Result: the tooth is thinner at the top and thicker in the middle. The up-curve and the straight down-stroke do not overlap, so the two strokes double the width there [5:44].
4. Break about half a nuqta [7:49].
5. The curve at the break points towards the top of the previous tooth. The next one does the same. Together they look like a flipped heart [7:49].
6. Third letter: here a final Ta' (in Bayt), with its end curve [8:19].
- The run along the bottom is a straight line, no ups and downs [8:19].
- The small dips between teeth are curves (down then up), not a drop and a sharp angle [9:21].

Warnings:
- The small cuts on the board are only where the teacher paused; they are not part of the shape [8:19].

### Four in a row: short, tall, medial curved tooth, end [9:52]
Steps:
1. First tooth 1 nuqta; second 3 nuqta, curved left, then straight down; break; 1 nuqta gap.
2. Third tooth (connected on both sides) changes shape [10:23]: it has a distinct curve, gets thinner going down, then goes back to the left. This is the same shape used later for Seen and Sheen.
3. Fourth: the end letter.
- How the medial curved tooth differs from the tall one [24:19]: the previous letter rises and stops sharply (like the end of a Ba'a); the tooth starts from that sharp stop and at once curves downward. The tall tooth goes up, stops, and at once comes down in a straight-ish line.

Warnings:
- The thin medial tooth is only for short connections. Do not elongate after it. (Seen has an elongated form, but this does not apply to Ba'a.) [15:28]

Unclear: [10:23] and [19:38] the exact curve of the medial tooth; [24:19] the teacher says to look at large examples because the video does not show it well.

### Full-width Ba'a inside a word (elongation) [12:25]
Used when a word needs more space (example built towards a word with Noon, Ya' and ta marbuta (?)).

2. Add a full Ba'a body of 6 nuqta, started like a normal Ba'a, dropping all the way and stopping [13:57].
3. The end of a connected Ba'a is higher than its start [14:27].
4. Connect onward to a Ya' tooth, which changes into the thin shape [14:27].
5. Optionally end with ta marbuta.

### Summary of Ba'a teeth [17:02]

- Three in a row: 1 / 3 / 1 nuqta, 1 nuqta between.

## Lesson 04: Jeem / Ha' / Kha' (overview of forms; isolated Ha' with straight head and open body; initial Ha' before ordinary letters; tooth joining into Ha')

Jeem, Ha' and Kha' are the same shape; only the letter dot differs (Jeem below, inside the body; Kha' above) [0:00], [18:38].

### Overview of the forms [0:00]
- Isolated: two heads (straight head, or a triangular head made of several moves) times two bodies (open body going right, or a body that rotates and comes back). Four combinations.
- Initial: (a) the generic one, which goes down with 1 nuqta of slope (the isolated head is almost straight); (b) the "almond" Ha', before a tall letter (it is closed up like an almond); (c) a straight-up, sharp-angled form, before letters that go down (another Ha', Ya', Meem).
- Medial: after Ba'a or Lam, joined from the top of the Ha'. Its end depends on what follows.
- This lesson: isolated (straight head, open body), initial generic, and the tooth that joins into Ha'.

### Isolated Ha', straight head, open body [4:38]
Measurements:
- Head width: 5 nuqta (always 5 for Ha') [4:38], [14:54].
- Height: 5 nuqta [13:53].
- The lower body sticks out 1 nuqta past the head on the right. Some calligraphers use 1.5; the teacher uses 1 [7:42], [11:18].
- Lower edge: 1 nuqta gap, measured from the left edge [9:14].

Head steps:
1. First move [4:38]: start just below the top line, near the last nuqta of the 5. Go down, then left. It is long: it runs out past the scale and stops at about the angle of the scale dots. Its top edge slopes slightly. (Some calligraphers make it flat, with no slope.) [5:38]
2. Second move [5:38]: from that edge go straight back up, then come back. This small decoration breaks the plain start.
3. Then go along with a very slight angle, curving up a little, back to the start point [6:09].
- What matters: the first move plus the second must be long [7:12].

Body steps [7:42]:
4. Start back from the head, leaving 1 nuqta. Go straight left, then down, then back. The usual break is at about the third nuqta [8:43].
5. As you go left, turn the pen to a more upright (vertical) angle [8:43], [12:19].
6. The left end should be a sharp angle, so it flows smoothly into the lower part. It is not an angular joint [9:14].
7. Come back along the bottom. Do not curve upward. Go back slightly straight-ish. The curve in the lower part gives the feeling of rising, not a pen movement upward [10:48].
8. Finish with a rotation of the pen, and let the end stick out 1 nuqta to the right [9:47], [12:19].
- The inside (negative space) is like a loaf of bread, not a circle [8:12], [10:17].
- The curve starts lower than you expect, and the change between the two curves starts a bit higher than usual [10:17].
- You need not restart the body from the head; you can start from a point lower down for better control [11:49].

Warnings:
- The inside must not be round [8:12].
- Do not curve the bottom upward with the pen [10:48].

Unclear: [5:08] the angle the first move ends at ("this angle of the dots"); [6:09] the direction of the closing pass of the head; [12:19] the "thick because I showed you the cuts" remark on pen angle; [9:14] which edge the 1-nuqta lower gap is measured from.

### Initial Ha' before ordinary letters (generic) [14:24]
Used before letters that neither rise nor drop.

Measurements: 5 nuqta wide; 1 nuqta of drop outside on the right; 1 nuqta below to set the drop; 1 nuqta inside [14:54], [16:26], [19:10].

Steps:
1. First move: go up a little first, so the decoration stays small [15:24].
2. Curve over and end the head. In three parts: curve up, curve back, then a straight line [15:55].
3. Go down: the 1 nuqta below makes you go down, not up. Break at about that point [16:57].
4. Keep moving, then curve back. This curve goes the other way [16:57].
5. Break and go on into the next letter. The next letter starts past the Ha' edge (for a Ba'a, at that edge) [17:28].
- It is wider than the straight-head form, which is shallower [17:28].
- Example: Habb / Jabb: up, up a bit, down 1 nuqta, back and down another 1 nuqta, back, then Ba'a [18:38].
- Do not use it before a tall letter that goes back up; keep that for the almond form (lesson 06) [19:10].

Unclear: [16:26] where the three sections fall; [17:28] which edge the next letter must come after.

### Tooth joining into Ha' from the top (medial Ha' after Ba'a/Ya') [19:40]
Whenever you join into Ha' from its top edge you must use this tooth. It is based on Ya'.

Measurements: tooth 3 nuqta high; its top part 2 nuqta high and 1 nuqta wide; 1 extra nuqta to guide the curve, like the Alef helia; the Ha' run is 3 nuqta long [19:40], [21:12]; leave 1 nuqta, and the end sticks out 1 nuqta [21:45].

Steps:
1. Start at the midpoint. Go up, curving to the left (a tusk shape). Stop [20:11].
2. Come back down along the same curve [20:41].
3. You may turn the pen slightly here: it is vertical going up, then changes [20:41].
4. Curve the other way, to the left, into the Ha'. This part is a very thin line [21:12], [22:45].
5. The Ha' head is straight-ish, curving slightly down. Leave 1 nuqta and stick out 1 nuqta [21:45].
6. At the end of a word: just go down (isolated body). If something follows: drop 1 nuqta and connect as in the initial form [22:15].
- Practice: Yahib (?) / words with Ya' + Jeem + Kaf; Habib [24:20], [25:59].

Unclear: [20:41] exactly when the pen angle changes; [23:16] "this has to be stuck from here" (where the tooth meets the Ha'); [24:20] garbled practice words.

## Lesson 05: Jeem / Ha' / Kha' continued (triangular head; the body that comes back; initial triangular-head Ha' before descending letters)

Heads and bodies mix: the triangular head can take the open body from lesson 04, and the straight head can take the returning body from this lesson [0:00], [14:26].

### Isolated Ha', triangular head [0:31]
Measurements:
- Whole head: 5 nuqta, as before [1:02].
- First move: 2 nuqta wide [1:02].
- Take 3 nuqta down from the start: leave 1 nuqta for the second move at the top, then 3 nuqta for the second move [1:02].
- 1 nuqta below the head to guide the comeback (it must go below the line) [3:07].

Steps:
1. First move [1:33]: start at the top, go down to the right, 2 nuqta wide, curving down to the right. Stop.
2. Second move [2:05]: from the start of the first move, go back and break: move the pen up and to the right.
3. Its top edge curves down slightly, not by much. It is a curve [2:37].
4. Stop. Check the shape by looking at the negative space [2:37].
- The left edge of the head is where the comeback must stop and turn [3:07].

Unclear: [1:02] how the "3 nuqta" are laid out (down or across); [2:05] where the second move starts.

### Returning body (comes back under itself) [3:37]
Measurements:
- Bottom edge has about a 1-nuqta curve [5:39].
- Lower part is 5 nuqta wide: from the third of the three nuqta, take 2 to the right and 2 to the left [6:39].
- The end climbs back up 2 nuqta [7:09].

Steps:
1. Hold the pen at a sharp angle. Curve down a little, then go left, while still moving downward [4:08].
2. The stroke gets thin here. The teacher says this comes from the way you hold the pen, not from the pen angle [4:08].
3. Keep going: a second curve, like the lower body in lesson 04. Keep moving down along an angled line [5:09].
4. Bottom: a 1-nuqta curve. Too sharp and it nears the angle above; too shallow and it comes back too soon [5:39].
5. The inside is an ellipse built from several curves [6:09].
6. Come back to the right, close to the line, then turn upward [6:39].
7. Curve upward, past the right edge a little, keeping an eye on the bottom line and the edge mark. Go up 2 nuqta. This rise is the "balancing feature" of this Ha' [7:09].
8. Moving up makes the thin edge on its own. Change direction smoothly; give the curves room; do not force anything [7:39].
9. Stop, and finish on the tip (corner) of the nib so the end narrows. Or use the thick part to make it easier [8:11].
- In summary: thin while going down, then thicker following the ellipse, then moving back up [8:42].

Move list as the teacher counts it [11:49], [25:53]:
1. down (first move of the head);
2. up with a curve that comes back (second move);
3. down, ending at the left side of the letter;
4. the bottom curve;
5. upward, slightly outside the right edge;
6. (in the second count) the joint between 4 and 5;
7. back, finishing the curve with the thin edge.
- The teacher sometimes writes the comeback side in one go because it feels natural [12:52].
- The lower part looks like the end of a Noon; the weight sits on the left [11:18].

Practice: do each move alone first with its scale (2 and 3 nuqta for move 1), then join them [8:42].

Unclear: [4:08] the "angle vs the way you hold the pen" remark; [6:39] which "three nuqta" the 2+2 is measured from; [12:22] "finishing it with an upper edge less like this one".

### Initial Ha' with triangular head, before descending letters [15:28]
Use it only before letters that go down (Ha', Jeem, Ya', Meem, Ra'). The head points down; an upward joint here would look like a tooth [22:16].

Measurements: 1 nuqta of drop with the 5-nuqta width [16:29]; 1 more nuqta below, and one more for full size [17:02]; 1.5 nuqta to the left where a following Ha' starts [18:04].

Steps:
1. Same first move as the isolated head [15:58].
2. Second move goes back and stops. The head drops sharply by 1 nuqta [16:29].
3. Go down along the line set by the nuqta marks into the next letter. The connection stops there [17:02], [17:33].

Following letter:
- **Ha'/Jeem after it** [18:04]: start 1.5 nuqta to the side. The two Ha's meet at an angle. The second Ha' head is the straight-head kind but straight-ish, not curving down much.
- **Ya' or Meem after it** [19:39]: go up, left, then right, down 1 nuqta, then curve back.
- **Ra' after it** [20:40]: the connection is shallower. Drop the Ra' 2 nuqta and add the Ra' scale (2 nuqta) behind it. Give the Ra' its full size; do not cut it short because part of it seems taken by the connection [21:13].
- **Ha' after a joined Ha'** [22:48]: the joint is a slight curve, not straight. Measured from a straight line it drops half a nuqta. The Ha' then curves down and back, 5 nuqta wide. Add 1.5 nuqta outside to get 1 nuqta inside [23:19].

Warnings:
- The final Jeem does not join onward in the middle of a word. Some calligraphers do it as an artistic choice; do not, for now [30:33].

Practice words: Hijab (drop 1, slant left, drop 1.5, then Alef, then a 6-nuqta Ba'a) [26:56]; Hajij (?) (Ha' into Jeem with this joint, Ya', final Jeem; dots: Jeem below, Ya' two dots) [27:59].

Unclear: [17:02] the order of the three nuqta marks; [23:19] "one and a half outside, one inside" shown on the board; [30:02] "leave one or two points ... two points for the right side" on the final Jeem.

## Lesson 06: Jeem / Ha' / Kha' continued (almond initial Ha' before rising letters; helia tooth into Ha'; summary of all Ha' cases)

### Initial "almond" Ha', before a rising letter [0:00]
Use it only when the next letter goes up (e.g. Alef, Lam). Named for the almond shape of its inside.

Measurements:
- 5 nuqta wide; for Jeem add 1 nuqta [0:31].
- A 1-nuqta mark outside the edge, dragged down as a rough guide [0:31].
- The first move's curve is barely half a nuqta, or less [1:33].
- Inside: 1.5 nuqta [2:03]. Outside: 1 nuqta (only a help; the inside marks matter most) [2:34].
- Height of the descending move: 3 nuqta [3:05].

Steps:
1. Hold the pen slightly inclined, not vertical (you may try vertical at first) [1:02].
2. First move: down to the right, then to the left. Two small curves, one each way. Do not overdo them [1:02].
3. Second move, as in the earlier Ha': go up with a curve, touch the top line, then start down [1:33].
4. Turn back and straighten the movement, with only a hint of an upward curve [1:33].
5. Go down. Break at the 1.5-nuqta mark [3:05].
6. Drop below, then start back up and connect to the Alef (or whatever follows) [3:05].
- Inside shape: a vertical line, a curve joined to it, then a line going back. It is more vertical than the inside of the generic initial Ha', which leans left [4:07].
- Practice idea: draw an almond and turn the pen around it [2:34].

Unclear: [1:02] "slightly inclined angle"; [3:05] where the 3 nuqta are measured; [3:36] the teacher says the camera angle hides the vertical view.

### Tooth with helia joining into Ha' (Ba'a or Lam before Ha') [7:10]
Same rules if the tooth is a Lam. The Ha' part is the same Ha' as before; the new part is the tooth on top [10:49].

Measurements:
- Tooth: 3 nuqta high, leaning well to the right [7:40], [24:22]. (There is also a 2-nuqta tooth, taught later.)
- Helia starts after 1 nuqta [7:40].
- The Ha' reaches 2 nuqta to the left and 3 nuqta to the right of the tooth (teacher first said 3 left, then corrected) [8:45].
- Usually 1 nuqta of drop, and 1 nuqta of clearance inside [9:15].

Steps:
1. Helia first [7:40]: hold the pen at an angle and make a short stroke, smaller than the pen width (leave a quarter).
2. Turn the pen to vertical. Put the tip of the nib on the helia and move down [8:14].
3. Curve the first third to the right, then change the curve to the other way [8:14].
4. Run along the Ha' head to the left. This head is nearly horizontal: it goes left, not down [9:46].
5. Go up a little, then break down, as in the earlier Ha' [10:18].
6. Join the helia to the tooth to finish the head [9:46].
- This helia leans further right than the Alef's helia [10:18].

Warnings:
- The tooth must curve clearly to the right [24:22].

Unclear: [8:14] "leave a quarter" (a quarter of what); [10:18] "should have been a bit longer" (which part).

### The drop rule for a connected Ha' [11:20], [18:32]
- Nothing follows the Ha': just go down; no drop to think about.
- Something follows, and the Ha' top is straight: drop 1.5 nuqta outside to get 1 nuqta inside.
- Something follows, and the Ha' top is angled: 1 nuqta outside, then down and up.
- Warning: a too-shallow drop gives a thin, dead inside [11:51].

### Summary of Ha' cases [5:08], [19:33]
Isolated (4 forms; there are no others) [5:39], [12:21]:
- Straight head + open body (going right) or + returning body.
- Triangular head + open body or + returning body.
- The open end sticks out 1 nuqta; or use the returning end "for more beauty" [12:51].
- Triangular head: its top curve is only half a nuqta; then come back keeping 1 nuqta inside [13:21].

Initial (3 forms, chosen by the next letter) [6:09], [14:22]:
1. Generic: down 1 nuqta, 5 nuqta wide, 1 nuqta inside, 1 nuqta to connect. Joins to anything.
2. Triangular head: joins to a descending letter, from the top; drop more; 1.5 nuqta on the left.
3. Almond: joins to a rising letter. The vertical part curves down, then up; 1 nuqta here, 1 nuqta there [15:55].

Medial (joined from the top) [16:27]:
1. After a tall tooth that rises and turns back (lesson 04).
2. After a Ra'-like joint (taught with Ra') into a slightly vertical Ha'.
3. After the helia tooth from this lesson; drop more, 1 nuqta.
- Joining a Ha' "normally" from the bottom like other scripts is very rare in Thuluth [17:28].

Practice words [20:05]:
- Janib: almond Jeem, 1 nuqta, a 1-nuqta Noon tooth, then Ba'a. Dots: Noon, Jeem, Ba'a.
- A word with Ya', Ta', final Jeem ("Yatij" (?)): helia tooth, Ha' head 2 nuqta straight, final Jeem drops 2 nuqta and is 4 nuqta wide; the Ha' joint drops half a nuqta; round end [21:12].

Unclear: [20:41] "the distance after the Ha'"; [22:44] the final Jeem's 2-nuqta drop and 4-nuqta width; [23:21] the practice word itself.

## Lesson 07: Dal / Dhal (isolated Dal; Dal connected from the right)

Dal and Dhal are the same shape; Dhal adds one letter dot on top [0:00], [6:44].

### Isolated Dal [0:00]
Measurements:
- Three nuqta side by side: the first limits the first move; the next marks the other side of the Dal. A horizontal line on top [0:00].
- Helia: 1 nuqta wide [1:03].
- Second move: 2 nuqta of drop, but it goes below these, so it is more than 2 nuqta tall [1:33].
- Third move: 4 nuqta wide (marked from the right side); the line dropped down meets the half-nuqta mark [3:09].
- 2 nuqta of height below the third move [3:41].
- Gap at the end: 2 nuqta (some people use 1.5) [5:12].

Steps:
1. Helia (first move) [0:31]: put the lower edge of the nib on one line and the top edge on the other. Move down to the right and rotate the pen so the stroke closes on itself. This makes a triangular shape.
2. Second move (the back) [2:06]: put the pen at the midpoint. Pen roughly vertical. Curve down. The first curve is smooth, not obvious.
3. Break, then a second curve that is curvier [2:06], [2:37].
- The upper curve is long and the lower one is short. This contrast breaks boredom [2:37].
4. Third move (the foot) [3:41]: a very steep drop. Move straight down, touch the line, then curve.
5. The curve turns around one nuqta. It is the sharpest curve in the script [3:41].
6. Keep curving. The end points towards the top of the letter. Stop where it meets the right side [4:11], [4:42].
- Inside (negative space): a clear area, then a sharp line [4:42].
- Fast version: down, smooth curve, a curvier curve, down, curve back up [5:43].

Warnings:
- If the foot does not go down far enough, the letter does not feel right [14:23].
- Dal never connects forward. A following letter may touch it; that is usually a touch, not a connection [6:44].

Unclear: [1:03] which two lines the nib edges sit on for the helia; [1:33] exact height of the second move; [5:43] "I need to cut it from here" (trimming the foot); [5:43] pen angle "you can see the angle I'm using".

### Dal connected from the right (final Dal) [7:14]
Seen in the name of the Prophet (Muhammad) [10:19].

Measurements:
- 5 nuqta of height above the connecting curve: the Dal is about 3, plus 2 to reach its start [7:14], [7:44].
- 1 nuqta added on top where the stroke turns upright [7:44].
- 4 nuqta of drop [8:15].
- 2 nuqta of drop below; it goes below the connection [9:19].
- Inside: marks 2, 3 and 4 [9:19].

Steps:
1. Come from the connection, go up and curve to the left [8:15].
2. Two curves: rotate around the outer one first, then the inner one, as with Alef and Ba'a [8:15].
3. Come down in a straight-ish line, at an angle, for 4 nuqta. As with the tall Ba'a tooth: up-curve then straight down, so the stroke is thick in the middle and thin at the top and bottom [8:48].
4. Break before the end [8:48].
5. Drop down to the left with another curve, closing the Dal on itself [11:19].
6. As with the isolated Dal: touch the line, curve back. The end points to the tip of the Dal and stops at the edge [9:49], [10:19].
- It is slightly curvier than the isolated Dal [10:19].
- Dropping below the connection is a mark of this script [9:19].

Unclear: [9:49] "this is actually too long, we might want to just rotate it down here".

### Practice [11:51]
- Jad / Had: almond Jeem (lesson 06) with equal sides inside; Dal starts 1 nuqta after it, rises left, drops 2 nuqta, curves back [12:21].
- Hadhiq (?) ("clever"): each letter balances on the one before. These small touches matter in Thuluth [13:21].
- Dubb ("bear"): Ba'a then Dal; the other Ba'a (up, down, curves back) also works [14:23].

## Lesson 08: Ra' / Zay (three isolated forms; Ra' connected after Ba'a; Ra' after Ha')

Ra' and Zay are the same shape; Zay adds one letter dot on top [0:00], [15:48]. Ra' has three main shapes. Forms 1 and 2 share the same start; form 3 is quite different [1:02].

### Shared start for forms 1 and 2: helia + tooth [0:32]
Measurements: a tooth (Ba'a-like) 2.5 nuqta high [0:32], [2:37]. Helia 1 nuqta wide; start it half a nuqta in (the tooth is 1 nuqta from the edge, but the helia takes half) [5:13].

Steps:
1. Helia: start at the top and make the triangular shape, as for Dal. Stop [0:32].
2. Put the pen at the top again, at a fairly vertical angle. Move down to the right. Stop [1:02].

### Isolated Ra' form 1: long open tail [1:34]
Measurements: 6 nuqta wide; 2 nuqta of drop on the right of the letter [1:34], [2:37].

Steps:
1. From the tooth, go down with a slight curve (inside it is a slight curve) [2:05].
2. When you touch the drop line, start rotating the pen about its own axis so the stroke narrows [2:05].
3. Continue the long ending; it thins out [2:37].
- Shape: a curve to the right, then a smooth change downward [2:37].

Warnings: too thin at the end is wrong [3:09]. It should be smoother than the board example [2:05].

Unclear: [2:05] where exactly the rotation starts.

### Isolated Ra' form 2: tail curves back up [3:09]
Measurements: 4 nuqta wide (instead of 6); 2 nuqta of drop; 1 nuqta below to force the curve down; the tip rises back to 1 nuqta below the top [3:09], [4:12].

Steps:
1. Same helia and tooth [3:42].
2. Curve down 2 nuqta, 4 nuqta wide [4:12].
3. Curve back up, all the way to 1 nuqta below the top [3:42].
- It looks like the end of a Noon [3:42].

Warnings: if it does not drop the full 2 nuqta it looks wrong; this one should be longer [4:42].

### Isolated Ra' form 3: no helia, rises first [5:13]
Measurements:
- Start: 2 nuqta wide, starting 1 nuqta below the top edge [5:47].
- 2 nuqta below that 1 nuqta: they set the drop [5:47].
- Total width: 5 nuqta [6:49], [7:22].
- 2 more nuqta mark where the last bit ends [7:22].

Steps:
1. Go up, then curve down, over 2 nuqta. The curve leans to the right; it is not even on both sides [6:18].
2. Drop all the way down to the two drop marks [6:18].
3. Around the last two nuqta, start rotating the pen before you reach the point [6:49].
4. Rotate and come back. The pen narrows [7:22].

## Lesson 20: Kaf (medial Kaf, two shapes; review of all Kaf forms; final Kaf)

2. Move 1 is the head serif: go up and to the left. Finish the head as a triangle; the stroke runs out of it.
3. Go down. Curve very slightly to the left, then back to the right. The downstroke has two gentle curves.
4. Move into the bowl. It drops 2 nuqta, the same move as the bowl of Seen and Sheen.
5. Curve up. The end stops before (below) the top of the Noon's tooth.

Measurements:
- Start: 0.5 nuqta from the edge.
- Tooth: 3 nuqta high.
- Bowl drop: 2 nuqta.
- Bowl inside width: 5 nuqta.
- 1 nuqta of drop in the bowl's curve "on the inside".

Warning:
- The downstroke's two curves are very slight.

### Final Noon [1:33]
1. Come in from the left (the previous letter) and stop.
2. Write the Seen/Sad bowl and its final move.

Note: the final Noon's bowl is no different from the bowls of Seen, Sheen and Sad. The isolated Noon only adds the Ba'a-like tooth in front.

### Noon after Ya'a or Ba'a [2:34]
- When a Ya'a or Ba'a joins a Noon, use the joining shape taught in the Ba'a lesson. One example comes down from a Lam, then Ya'a, then Noon.
- Another example [3:04]: a Lam comes from above, then a Ya'a, then a Noon. The Ya'a goes up 3 nuqta, then down into the Noon.
- Option [3:35]: the Noon can be cut short. The teacher compares this to a similar ending with a straight line, where the curvature is shallow. In the Noon the curvature is more visible.

### Medial Noon [4:05]
Use the same shapes as the medial Ba'a.

Example word "nahnu" (us): Noon, then Ha'a, then final Noon.
- Balance the letters. Imaginary lines run through the word so the shapes flow into each other.
- The final Noon's dot should read as a continuation of a nearby stroke (the next letter, perhaps a Dal (?)). It is not actually joined; it just sits comfortably.

### Trap: a seam where the stroke restarts [6:41]
- When you put the pen back down to carry on a letter, you may set it down slightly past the earlier stroke. This leaves a small break or notch.
- Fix: move the pen back a little, then forward, so the two parts meet smoothly with no gap.
- This is easier to see with a bigger pen.

## Lesson 26: Ha'a (the letter ه: isolated Ha'a, which doubles as Ta marbuta, and initial Ha'a)

Caption glossary: "ha"/"hat"/"heart" = Ha'a (ه); "Helia"/"hell yeah"/"Hillier" = head serif (?); "dots"/"Dodge" = nuqta; "timer"/"terminal border" = Ta marbuta; "down"/"dial"/"dull" = Dal; "far shape" = Fa'a-like loop (?).

### Isolated Ha'a (same shape as in the Lam-Alif; with two dots = Ta marbuta) [0:00]
Rough size guide: 5 nuqta.

1. Head serif: start on the left and go up to the left, as if making a dot. It looks like a triangle.
2. Move 1: come back, break (turn sharply), and go down to the right. Stop with the stroke end vertical.
3. Move 2: start inside, 0.5 nuqta in. Go down, touch, and rotate, then come back immediately. The turn is sharp.
4. Move 3, the last move [3:10]: rotate the pen. Go back up with a thin, smooth stroke, not the full thickness. Hold the pen at a different angle from the start.

Measurements:
- Overall guide: 5 nuqta.
- Move 1 ends about 1 nuqta higher (than the base (?)).
- 1 nuqta between the first move and the last move.
- 1 nuqta of space inside.
- Move 2 starts 0.5 nuqta in, and there is 0.5 nuqta "here".

Shape checks [1:35]:
- One part sticks further out than another, on both left and right.
- One part sits higher than the other.

Recap [3:40]: head serif, break with a curve to the right, stop at the bottom, break to the left and down, rotate the pen, and come back with a smooth move.

Notes:
- In the Lam-Alif this stroke comes in from a higher angle and is longer.
- Pen angle change: the last stroke uses a different pen angle so it comes out thin. Its end points one way while the first stroke points the other; the two are curved opposite ways.
- With two dots on top it is the Ta marbuta.

### Initial Ha'a [4:45]
Measurements:
- Drop 1 nuqta.
- Width 2 nuqta.
- Move 2 rises to 0.5 nuqta past (above) the top of move 1.
- The inner loop reaches up to the 1 nuqta mark after this move.

Steps:
1. Head serif.
2. Move 1: from the serif, extend, then go down to the right and stop at an angle. It is like a Dal, but the Dal is much longer.
3. Move 2: come back with a curve, curving down then up. This move goes upward, not downward. Keep going up until it rises 0.5 nuqta past the top of move 1.
4. Move 3: the inner loop, a "Fa'a-like" shape (?). Go back with a thin line, up to the 1 nuqta mark.
5. Change the pen angle for move 3. For move 2 the pen is quite vertical; here it is slightly to the left.
6. Use the lower side (corner) of the pen so the loop does not stick out past move 1.
7. At the top, lift the pen [7:53].
8. Move 4: write the inner shape, run down to "here" at this angle, and go back out.

Recap [9:58]: head serif, down 2 nuqta (?), rotate and back up, run outside, go up with a thin line, hold the pen higher, go back, and exit.

Warnings:
- The negative space of 1 nuqta width "here" should match the space "there" [8:24]. Both spaces must be similar.
- With the full pen thickness, the loop would stick out past the first move.

Style variation [8:55]: at the turning point, some write the return stroke horizontal, others at a slight angle. Some keep it shallow, others exaggerate it so the letter points right. The teacher goes "in the middle" (?).

Finishing [11:32]: carry the head serif on as a triangle and touch it up afterwards.

Unclear:
- [1:03] "we need to end this typically one point higher": higher than what.
- [1:35] Which parts stick out and which sit higher: needs the board.
- [5:46] "over here you're gonna do a far shape up here": read as a Fa'a-like loop (?).
- [7:21] to [7:53] The pen lift is stated once, with no detail on where the next stroke starts.
- [8:24] "this one it would have to go down to here": which space matches which.
- [9:58] "down for the two points": conflicts with the 1 nuqta drop given at [5:15]. May be the width.

## Lesson 27: Ha'a (medial Ha'a, five shapes)

Caption glossary: "hat"/"heart"/"her"/"huh" = Ha'a (ه); "nusk" = Naskh; "but"/"butt"/"bar" = Ba'a; "gene"/"gym" = Jeem; "mean"/"meme" = Meem; "yeah move" = Ya'a move; "fat"/"far" = Fa'a-like loop (?).

### Medial Ha'a 1: the general shape (as in Naskh) [0:00]
The teacher calls this the go-to medial Ha'a.

Guide measurements (reference points only):
- Height 3 nuqta.
- Drop 2 nuqta.
- Width 1.5 nuqta.

Steps:
1. The previous letter ends. Start the Ha'a from there, leaving a long enough run for the lower part and for the letter before.
2. Move 1: go down from the start, outside the 1.5 and 2 nuqta guides. It is fairly straight, with little curve.
3. Move 2: rotate and go back up. This stroke is quite curvy. At the top, make a slight, barely visible move. Stop just before the guide, because the lower part sticks out more than the upper part.
4. Move 3: go up to "here".
5. Move 4: go down. Start in from the second top point and go inside.

Shape notes:
- Move 1 must stay visible.
- Move 4 curves; the teacher exaggerates it to show which move he means.
- The lower part sticks out more than the upper part.
- The inside space is curvier than move 1.

Order said aloud at [2:01]: one, two, up to three, coming back for four.

### Medial Ha'a 2: open, entered from above [3:33]
Needs a letter that joins from the top, e.g. a Ba'a written to come down, or a Jeem. The stroke arrives going downward, not upward.

Measurements:
- A "4 nuqta" Ha'a (?); its lower part is 2 nuqta.
- Leave 1 nuqta.

Steps:
1. Drop 2 nuqta and stop.
2. Leave 1 nuqta, then come back up.
3. Curve one way. At the top, curve the other way.
4. Continue into any next letter.

Warning [5:04]:
- This is not a Meem. The Meem goes back sharply, with a flat surface. Here the shape "stands" upright.

### Medial Ha'a 3: entered from above, with a closing knot [5:34]
Same as shape 2 plus a closing move.

1. Come down from the letter above: drop 2 nuqta and stop.
2. Go up and make a nice knot. This combines shape 1 and shape 2.
3. Run the stroke outside, 1.5 nuqta.
4. Come back down and connect up into the next letter.

Recap: down 2, up, back down, connect.

The teacher says this is the hardest of the medial shapes [7:06].

### Medial Ha'a 4: two moves, a more upright Meem-like shape [7:06]
- Just two moves.
- Like the Meem already learned, but more vertical than horizontal.
- No measurements given.

### Medial Ha'a 5: based on the initial Ha'a, entered from above [7:39]
1. Come in from the top, e.g. after a Ba'a or Jeem.
2. Make a Ya'a move: go down with two curves.
3. Then something similar to a Ba'a.
4. Go 1.5 nuqta outside.
5. Come back with a small Fa'a-like loop and carry on.

How it differs from the initial Ha'a (Lesson 26):
1. The start is steeper and "not as pretty".
2. This stroke goes downward; in the initial Ha'a it goes upward.
3. The loop starts outside and turns outside. The turn from move 3 to move 4 happens outside. In the initial Ha'a it happens inside the top of move 1, which keeps the loop contained.

### Word practice [12:51]
Lam + Lam + Ha'a.
- Mark 2 nuqta "down here" and 3 nuqta "up there", plus a 1.5 nuqta measure.
- Rotate and come back.
- Keep the small nudge at the bottom.
- Finish with any Meem.

Unclear:
- [1:00] "outside the one and a half and outside the two what we're gonna do we're gonna go back downward": where move 1 starts relative to the guides.
- [1:30] "starting going in from the second top point": needs the board.
- [4:04] "it is a four point huh": 4 nuqta of what (height?).
- [7:06] Shape 4 is described only by pointing.
- [7:39] "you're gonna do a yeah move which is something we still need to talk about": the Ya'a move was not taught yet at this point.
- Pen lifts and pen-angle changes are not stated in this lesson.

## Lesson 28: Ha'a (final Ha'a, two shapes)

Caption glossary: "her"/"heart"/"hat"/"huh" = Ha'a (ه); "nursery" = Naskh (?); "done"/"Dell"/"dal" = Dal; "color" = fill in.

The teacher says there are three final shapes. Two are for normal writing; the third comes later.

### Final Ha'a 1: hooked (Naskh-like) [0:34]
Measurements:
- Climb before the letter: 1 nuqta, vertical.
- Drop below the line: 1 nuqta.
- 2 nuqta between the low point and the top of the return.
- "We've got 1 nuqta here and 2.2" [5:49] (see Unclear).

Steps:
1. Come up from the previous letter, rising 1 nuqta vertically.
2. Stop suddenly and change direction. This shows where the letter starts.
3. Rotate and drop back down, 1 nuqta below the writing line.
4. Rotate the pen and come back up to the line.
5. The return curves one way at first, the other way in the middle, then the first way again.
6. Fill in ("color") the last bit and the edges. The teacher calls this "two strokes together".

Pen / nib notes:
- The thickness falls steadily through the letter.
- At the start use the full nib width.
- Lower down use about two-thirds. The teacher says "you won't use the full thickness".
- Keep narrowing until only the tip of the pen is drawing. This makes a hook.
- When you lift the pen at the end it leaves a small gap at the bottom. You fill that in afterwards.
- At [4:16]: "we're going to lift the pen and rotate up, you see the thickness here it dropped". Whether "lift" means off the paper or only onto the corner is unclear.

Warnings:
- The end should point straight up, or slightly to the left.
- The hook must drop below the line. Do not draw it above the line.

### Final Ha'a 2: Dal-based, with a nose-like end [6:19]
Measurements:
- Move 1 height: 3 nuqta.
- Leave 1 nuqta below.
- Width: almost 2 nuqta.
- The final stroke is about 0.5 nuqta thick.
- It ends 1 nuqta lower than the reference point, which makes 2 nuqta of drop.

Steps:
1. Move 1: from the previous letter, go up and to the left, angled like a Dal (the teacher also calls it "a nail kind of thing" (?)). Stop.
2. Move 2: go straight down, Dal-like, with the Dal's thickness, leaving 1 nuqta below.
3. Break (turn sharply) so the letter sits on the guide nuqta.
4. Move 3: curve back. This rotation is curved, not straight.
5. Move 4: a double curve. Rotate the pen to a flatter, shallower angle so the stroke comes out about 0.5 nuqta thick.
6. End lower than the reference point.

Pen notes:
- Pen angle change before move 4. If you keep the angle you used for moves 2 and 3, the end comes out too thick and straight.

Warnings [9:24]:
- Watch the negative space inside.
- Watch the drop at the bottom.
- Lean left at the start.
- Change the pen angle; the end should be smooth "like a nose".
- No straight line at the end; it must be curved.

### Spacing [11:15]
- Leave 1 nuqta of space between the final Ha'a and the letter before it.
- For practice, stop, go down and come back to use shape 1 in a word.

Unclear:
- [2:11] "until you reached at this line up to here, the distance between these two would be two points": which two points.
- [5:49] "we've got one point here and we've got 2.2": possibly "2 by 2" (?).
- [6:19] "it's kind of like a nail of thing": garbled.
- [9:24] "it should end lower than this one and you need one point of this": the reference is not named.
- [11:45] "you've got the one two and you've got the half point of angle here": garbled recap.

## Lesson 29: Ya'a (isolated Ya'a, elongated Ya'a, the "dagger/sword" Ya'a, Ya'a after a tooth, spacing)

Caption glossary: "yeah"/"year"/"yacht" = Ya'a; "noon"/"new" = Noon; "nebula"/"number" = probably the nabra, the tall tooth stroke (?); "teeth" = tooth; "Pharisee" = Farsi (Nasta'liq) (?); "chip" = an unwanted blunt end (?); "color" = fill in.

### Isolated Ya'a (basic) [0:30]
Measurements:
- Guide: 1 nuqta mark at the start.
- Move 2: 2 nuqta long, about 2.5 nuqta drop. Use the 2 nuqta guide on the right for where to stop, and the 2 nuqta width for how far left to go.
- Tail (Noon-based): 2 nuqta high, 5 nuqta wide. The tail's return sits 3 nuqta from the head shape.

The head, move 1: a dot [1:03]
1. Set the pen on the 1 nuqta mark at its full thickness.
2. Move up toward the corner.
3. Stop, rotate, and come back. So move 1 is really a dot.

The head, move 2: the down-curve [1:39]
1. Curve downward.
2. It sits neatly on the first move and drops roughly 2.5 nuqta, "which is basically the one point from the teeth" (?).
3. It turns the opposite way to move 1 and stops just before the end of move 1.
4. The teacher says the pen "would keep its same angle throughout the shape" [3:12]. It is unclear whether "the shape" means move 2 only or the whole head.

The head, move 3: the S-curve [3:12]
1. Two curves joined. First one goes down: a sag curve.
2. The second straightens the letter: a crest curve, then stop.
3. The join between the two is fairly sharp.

The tail: a Noon move [3:43]
1. "Take the pen, put it here" at the start of the tail.
2. Go down like a Noon and touch the line.
3. Start rotating and come back up.
4. Stop at the marked point.
5. Because the return is 3 nuqta from the head, go further left and give it more curve.
6. The end must curve and point to the right. It does not go straight up.

Repeat without guides [5:18]: "one" (imagine a dot), "two" (smooth), "three" ("this has to be more vertical"). Again: one, two going down, "restart the rotation" while watching both curves, then the Noon move down, rotate, and come back.

Pen notes:
- The pen keeps one angle "throughout the shape" [3:12]: for move 2 or for the whole head (unclear).
- The tail rotates the pen as it turns back up.

Unclear:
- Pen lifts: the teacher never says outright whether the pen lifts between move 1 (dot), move 2 and move 3.
- [3:43] "Take the pen put it here" before the tail suggests a fresh pen placement, so possibly a lift between the S-curve and the tail. This is not confirmed.
- [5:51] to [6:23] "restart the rotation" may mean a new stroke or just a pen turn.
- [1:39] "this will just sit neatly on this one": what move 2 sits on.
- [2:11] "which is basically the one point from the teeth over here": garbled.
- [5:51] What "this has to actually be more vertical" refers to (move 2 or move 3).

### Elongated Ya'a [6:53]
For when a long Ya'a is needed, e.g. to hold a word in the middle.

1. Write the same head: moves 1, 2 and 3.
2. Tail: based on the Noon. It sags 2 nuqta and is 12 nuqta wide (counted "four ... twelve").
3. Go down with a slight curve. Practise by drawing a straight line and curving around it. You can split it into chunks.
4. Stop, then curve back the other way.
5. Just before the end point, start rotating the pen.
6. Fill in the end.

Measurements: sag 2 nuqta, width 12 nuqta.

### "Dagger/sword" Ya'a (the backward-tail shape, same as used with Ba'a before those letters) [8:26]
Measurements:
- Drop 2 nuqta.
- Shoulder 3 nuqta wide.
- 1 nuqta of height at the back, made by the tail thinning out.

Steps:
1. Go up in a dagger shape. Hold the pen near vertical.
2. Rotate back with a little kick backwards.
3. Change the pen angle to flat and shallow as you reach the turn. If you keep the vertical angle, the end comes out blunt ("chip"), which is wrong.
4. Move back with a slight curve downward, then upward: a sword shape.
5. The second move curves slightly downward, then goes down immediately.
6. Go down, curve up, curve downward, then go up using this side of the pen at the end.

Note: it resembles the Farsi (Nasta'liq) Ya'a.

Pen notes:
- Pen angle changes inside the stroke: near-vertical for the dagger, then flat for the smooth finish.
- The end thins using the pen's side.

Unclear:
- [10:30] "it's like a one point like a two point actually": 1 or 2 nuqta.
- [11:00] "using this size right at the end": side or size of the pen.

### Ya'a after a tall tooth (nabra (?)) [12:35]
Measurements:
- Tooth: 3 nuqta high.
- Keep 1.5 nuqta to the left of the tooth for a smooth join.
- About 0.5 nuqta of drop on the joining stroke.

Steps:
1. Write the tooth: start here and go left, then back.
2. Joining stroke: a slight curve. Run it outside and keep it flat.
3. Extend it slightly at the end.
4. Second move: not the same as in the isolated Ya'a. It is nearly straight, with only a slight downward curve. Begin by doing it straight and add the curve with practice.
5. Finish with the normal Noon move. Rotate and come back, ending at the angle of the isolated Ya'a.

Warning:
- Do not let the joining stroke go down; it is only about 0.5 nuqta of drop.

### Tooth + dagger Ya'a [15:11]
1. Go up, then down.
2. Leave 2 nuqta under the letter.
3. Come back with the shape of the isolated Ya'a.
4. Curve down, then up.
5. Finish with a stop. The upward curve is mirrored by a curve below.

Applies with or without dots (Ya'a, Ba'a and the like).

### Ya'a joined from a letter above (e.g. "Ali") [17:17]
- Keep the 2 nuqta curve down and up.
- Work out the height so the Ya'a does not hit the letter above, and is not so low that it looks wrong.
- The Ya'a drops 1 nuqta below, to balance the stroke above it [20:26].

### Spacing [18:19]
- Most letters: leave 1 nuqta between the letter and the Ya'a.
- Some letters need 2 nuqta.
- After a Meem leave at least 2 nuqta, because the Meem pulls the letter back.
- Usual drop: 1 nuqta.

Unclear:
- [12:35] "nebula": read as the tall tooth (nabra) (?).
- [13:39] "this is more of a half pointed drop": read as 0.5 nuqta.
- [15:43] "connect the three-point nebula to these sodium there that goes back": read as the dagger/backward Ya'a (?).
- [18:50] What "this chip" refers to in the spacing demo.

## Lesson 30: Lam-Alef (two standalone forms, the connected form, and the "rabbit" ending for ta marbuta / ha)

The teacher treats Lam-Alef as one letter built from three moves: an alif move and two new moves. One of the new moves is a kaf move. The teacher compares it to a ta marbuta (goes down, turns, comes back) made very long.

### Standalone Lam-Alef, form 1 (alif + "banana" + long kaf-like stroke) [0:31]
Move 1: the alif [1:04]
1. Write it like a normal alif, but only 6 nuqta tall instead of 7.
2. Reason: 2 nuqta are needed for the other part of the shape lower down.
3. Start with the head (the teacher's "helia", the alif's starting hook): go up, come back, change the thickness, and finish down at the bottom. "Nothing new here."

Move 2: the "banana" [2:08]
1. Drag the pen downward with a 2-nuqta drop.
2. Move with the cut of the nib (the pen held the same way throughout), then start turning to the left.
3. Go down and add weight, then rotate the pen to reduce the thickness.
4. Lift and finish pointing downward. Small start, small finish.
5. Width: 3 nuqta. Measure it from the far edge, because that is where the last move will connect. The 3 nuqta start "from this edge".
6. Go really slowly.
7. Practice help [4:11]: if it is hard, draw a straight line connecting the two ends, draw another line next to it, then fill the space with the pen.

Move 3: the long kaf-like stroke [4:43]
1. It starts 4 nuqta away from the lam (the alif of move 1) and drops half a nuqta.
2. Start with the head, as for the kaf. Then break downward.
3. Body: the first two-thirds is a sagging (concave) curve. The last third curves back the other way (garbled "crystal", probably "crest", a convex curve (?)).
4. It ends floating just over the guide line. It must not touch it. The guide lines should cross at the finish point.
5. The head is 1.5 nuqta. Continue it and cut it off where it measures about 1.5 nuqta.

Finishing [7:48]: excess ink can be scraped off with a cutter or a sharp tool once it dries.

Practice advice [8:50]: do each move on its own, focus on the dimensions, then combine the three moves. That is how you get consistent results.

### Standalone Lam-Alef, form 2 (the crossing form) [9:21]
1. Guides: a top line, a second line 1 nuqta below it, and a guideline 4 nuqta wide.
2. First move (the lam): like the last move of form 1. Start with the head and go up a little more. Come down two-thirds of the way, then finish.
3. Add a shape "exactly the same as" the ta marbuta (?) down below. It leaves 1 nuqta of space inside.
4. Last move (new) [10:26]:
   - Hold the pen at a fixed angle and move along the nib's cut.
   - The stroke has two curves: first a convex (crest) curve, then a concave (sag) curve.
   - The thickness can only change within a "really narrow window". This produces the thinnest line of the letter.
   - At the end, stop and leave the cut shape of the nib. Do not close or round off the end.
5. It keeps moving until it touches the top part. Its end is 4 nuqta from the midpoint. It sits higher than the previous move, then goes back up and stops [13:00].
6. This move reaches further right than the left move.
7. Practice [11:59]: start with more curve than you need, then reduce it. A drill is to stamp nib cuts in a chain ("up right, up right, up right") to build muscle memory for this stroke.
8. The teacher calls this harder than form 1. If you have practised kaf and ta enough, it will come.

### Connected Lam-Alef (after a preceding letter) [13:33]
1. When Lam-Alef connects, use the form-1 construction, but the lam becomes short, like a plain lam.
2. Example: kaf + Lam-Alef. Write the kaf first. Then go up with a small lam that touches the kaf, 2 nuqta tall.
3. On the way back, mark 2 nuqta, with one nuqta at each of two places, and extend the second move to reach about there [14:39].
4. Then mark 4 nuqta for the last move and the half-nuqta drop. Finish with the alif.
5. Move slowly when writing the lam.
6. A longer, connected version exists, but the teacher is "not a big fan" of it. He prefers to keep it for the standalone shape [15:43].

### Optional "rabbit" ending for standalone ta marbuta / ha [16:14]
1. The standalone ta marbuta (studied earlier) looks like a Lam-Alef: down, then back. Normally it ends at the bottom.
2. Option: instead of ending there, come back with an extra shape like the Farsi (Nasta'liq?) ending, which connects to the previous move.
3. Rotate back at 1 nuqta of height, then extend slightly and finish "just before here".
4. The same option applies when ha is connected, e.g. "Taha": up, down, up, then this move.

### Warnings / traps
- The alif in form 1 is 6 nuqta, not the usual 7.
- The last stroke of form 1 must float just above the line, not touch it.
- Form 2's last stroke ends square, with the nib's cut left visible. Do not close it.
- The teacher says the camera angle is poor and his own shapes are rough. Copy one master's model and do not mix masters.

- [5:44] "you're gonna need these lines to basically intersect": which lines is not stated.
- [6:16] "crystal" curve: probably crest (convex) (?).
- [6:47] "connect this to this and cut at this one": the 1.5-nuqta cut point needs the board.
- [9:55] "add a top or Buddha shape ... exactly the same as the thermal water": probably ta marbuta (?). "go back and review the guitar" is garbled.
- [13:00] "Four Points far from the midpoint": which midpoint is not stated.
- [14:39] The two marked nuqta for the connected form are board-dependent.
- [16:45] "a shape that looks like a pharmacy lot": probably the Farsi/Nasta'liq ending (?).

## Lesson 31: Diacritics (harakat, tanwin, sukun, shadda, madd, wasla, hamza, and the decorative fillers)

General pen rule [1:04]: there are two sizes. One is the same pen as the writing. The other is a pen one-third of the writing pen's width. "In general it's the third size." The teacher demonstrates large, but says that in real writing marks are generally a third the size of the pen [20:09].

### Fatha / kasra (short stroke) [0:33]
1. Guide: a 2-nuqta scale.
2. Hold the pen at a "nice inclination" and draw down until the stroke touches the lower line.
3. It needs no curve. It may curve slightly downward.
4. Do not make it very steep or flat. Keep it at "this angle".
5. Rough proportion: "take it six by six" (?) [1:04].
6. Pen: usually the third-width pen. With it the stroke comes out short.
7. Size check [2:06]: its width is about that of a waw (a 2-nuqta letter). A fatha sits on top of a waw.
8. The same stroke is used as fatha (above) and kasra (below).

### Long fatha (full pen) [3:10]
1. Same angle, moving downward. It is longer than the short one.
2. At the end, change shape, turn around and come back with almost 1 nuqta of width.
3. Use: it fills a large gap. The example is a word written as lam + ain + lam + ya (the transcript says "Ali" (?)). The space above it would look empty. A small fatha leaves it empty; the long one balances the word.
4. Choose by space. Use the full thickness where there is room. Use the short (third) one for kasra and where a full-size fatha cannot fit above the letters.

### Tanwin fath (double fatha) [4:40]
1. Write the fatha. Add a second one directly below it.
2. Gap between them: one third-pen thickness. "It's kind of tight."
3. Write the top one first, then the lower one, and balance the two.

### Tanwin kasr [5:11]
1. Shorter. Always third-pen, because tanwin is big and always comes at the end of a word.
2. It can also stand in for the fatha form when space is short.
3. Full-size tanwin is rare.

### Damma [6:13]
Three moves:
1. Go up to the left. It looks straight but is slightly curved on both sides.
2. Come back the other way to make a flat cap.
3. Go down at the same pen angle. Move slightly left to add thickness, then rotate the pen to close. Thick to thin. It must end pointing downward, "as if it was standing".

### Tanwin damm, form 1 (two dammas) [7:46]
Copy the damma twice. The second follows the first, sitting at about the same level.

### Tanwin damm, form 2 (connected) [8:16]
1. Up to the left, then moves 2 and 3 as for the damma, stopping at a set point.
2. Come back with a move like the Diwani dal. Go back, stop, go up-left, and make a head like the first one.
3. Close both moves onto each other.

### Tanwin damm, form 3 (with a ring) [9:19]
Same start: go down and stop. Hold the pen where the curve changes, go down, turn around, and make a ring around the head.
- Note: in the Quran, tajweed decides which form is used. One form is used with no idgham, the other when there is idgham. Other shapes exist that the teacher did not show [10:19].

### Sukun, form 1 [10:49]
1. Go down to the right like a dal, then come back.
2. Moves 1 and 2 are a downward move with a sharp curve. Move 3 is a straight line that closes it.
3. Posture: neither leaning back nor upright, in between.

### Sukun, form 2 (always full thickness) [11:23]
1. Up and down like a ha head (?). Then come back with the third-width pen, down-left (?).
2. The result is a "banana" shape below with a "hat" shape on top.
3. Use: over silent letters, e.g. the alif of "al-". It is a sign that the letter is silent, as against a hamza/wasla mark (?) [11:54].

### Shadda [12:25]
1. Curve, curve, then curve back like a lam. Then come back with a line down.
2. The second curve is thicker and wider than the first.
3. Come back and close.
4. A quick, simplified form is also seen.
5. Placement: with a fatha, the fatha goes above the shadda. With a kasra, the kasra goes below the shadda (?).
6. Trap [13:31]: keep sizes consistent. A small third-pen shadda next to a massive full-pen fatha looks wrong.

### Madd [13:58]
Used where an alif is pronounced but not written (example: between meem and noon in a word written without its alif), and for alif maqsura.
- Shape 1: go back with the third pen in a slight curve. Then return with a kaf head plus a bar, like a sin's stroke. Make the sin-like move, then stop.
- Shape 2 [15:34]: similar. Go back, stop, curve around and come back, but go all the way up, then return with a ha-like (?) shape.
- Both appear large, mostly in the middle of a word, because they stand in for a medial alif.

### Wasla (sila) over hamzat al-wasl [16:07]
1. A slide (sweep), then a nudge upward, then come back.
2. Never full thickness: it must fit above the alif. Always third size.

### Placement rules [17:08]
- Each letter has a "zone of influence". If a mark shifts right or left of its letter, it confuses the reader.
- Worked example (waw, meem, alif, lam, waw): put a small fatha in the narrow space. The meem can take a bigger fatha because the area around it is empty. Above the alif there is room for a large fatha or a wasla.
- Use a small mark where a big one would touch the letters on both sides.
- Mark position depends on the letter's location and width. Keep it close to, and above, its letter, balanced.
- Do not place a mark (e.g. a damma) where it pushes the next alif away and leaves a big gap between words [19:38].

### Hamza [20:09]
1. Shape like an ain head. Go up and back, break down, then connect to the lower shape.
2. Goes above the alif, below it, or on its own (?).

### Decorative fillers [21:10]
Use them to balance negative space.
- Leaf: go down, around and back. Usually in the top area.
- A second shape like a hamza or a small noon (?). It "comes everywhere".
- Mizan [21:42], four moves:
  1. Back up-left.
  2. Down, like the Arabic numeral one. Very thin.
  3. Curve around and come back up. Start with a gentle curve so it grows out of move 2. A straight start gives a sharp edge. Keep it thin; some people exaggerate its thickness.
  4. Come back. Join 3 and 4 smoothly by going slowly round the curve.
  - The whole sign is angled, pointing down-left. It may get a tail on either side, depending on the space.
- Second mizan: a small meem (down, back, thin) with a mizan sitting above it.
- Use: for large areas that cannot be filled. Ideally the letters themselves fill the gaps; fillers are for when that is really hard.

### Letter-disambiguation marks [24:15]
Small symbols next to sin, sad, ain and ha so they are not confused with similar letters.
- Sin mark: a small sin with a Farsi ending. Moves 1 and 2 point upward; move 1 is a short move down. Then a curve, a curve back, and down.
- Sad mark: a small sad shape (curve down, up) with a tail. Its sharp end goes back and rotates to close (?).
- Ha mark: "literally a [Farsi] connected to a [Ruq'a]" (?), a combination of Naskh, Ruq'a and Farsi shapes.
- In compositions (tarkib) the ha and ain marks are used most. The sin mark helps show a long stretched sin is a sin.

Unclear:
- [1:04] "take it six by six": the rough scale, needs the board.
- [8:47] Form 2 of tanwin damm ("like the diwani dull ... close both moves on each other") needs the board.
- [11:23] Sukun form 2: "just like a hat head script" may mean "like a ha head", and "down left unequloaded" is garbled.
- [11:54] Which mark (sukun vs "the circle"/wasla) goes on the alif of "al-" is garbled.
- [13:31] "if it was a third High it will come above her if it was Orchestra I will come below it": read as fatha above / kasra below the shadda (?).
- [14:02] Which word is the madd example (meem ... noon, alif not written) is garbled.
- [15:34] Madd shape 2: "return with a nice hat shape" (ha? hat?).
- [21:10] The second filler shape ("like the hems the num") is garbled.
- [24:45]-[25:50] All three disambiguation marks depend on the board. The descriptions are partly garbled.

## Lesson 32: "Allah" and "Muhammad" (the two special-case words, with their proportions)

### Allah (lafz al-jalala) [0:30]
Move 1: alif [0:30]
1. A normal 7-nuqta alif, written as usual: up, then down.
2. Next to it, mark 1 nuqta. This nuqta "governs your rotation" and is called an important skill [1:36].

Move 2: first lam [1:36]
1. Height 3 nuqta. The lam of this word is always shorter than the alif.
2. At the bottom it rotates down by 1 nuqta. It then takes a shape like the long ... (garbled "long nebulard", maybe the long ra/nun bowl (?)). It is more inclined than the usual one, which was half a nuqta.
3. Head [3:08]: short, 1 nuqta long. Make it like the dal head (or the nun's (?)), not the big alif head. Move right to reach the 1 nuqta, start thick, come back, then close the end.

Move 3: second lam [4:20]
1. Gap between the first and second lam: 1.5 nuqta at the bottom.
2. Keep the same pen angle. Do not curve downward; go straight up, "similar to the little ha".
3. Spacing: 1.5 nuqta at the bottom, 1 nuqta at the top.
4. Stop shorter than the first lam. Close the top.

Move 4: the connecting curve into ha [5:25]
1. Come back with a curve, like a lam connected to the next letter.
2. Leave 1 nuqta between the two curves. When rotating, leave a larger gap: about 2 nuqta at the bottom and 1.5 at the top.
3. Keep the ha's thickness steady while going back.
4. This curve dips more than the first one. If you drew a line along them, it would touch one and leave space behind the other. The word as a whole leans slightly, "but not by much".

Move 5: the ha [6:58]
1. Stop around the edge of the lam. Leave about 2 nuqta, then drop 1 nuqta for the ha.
2. Go down and stop around the midpoint, leaving half a nuqta.
3. Go back up and stop 1 nuqta lower than the previous move. The turn is sudden and thick, and it follows the previous curve. Keep the negative space small; do not swing out.
4. Make a sudden move down and around. Start changing the rotation the other way, and stop leaving half a nuqta (?).
5. End with a slight hook to the left, then snap back: "a nice nose", not a round curve.
6. This last part is less than full thickness. Lift the pen slightly on the way down so the stroke narrows to a neat finish.

Note [9:04]: a small alif is sometimes written between the lam and the ha, because the space between the two lams is too narrow for it.

### Muhammad [9:34]
Move 1: first meem (connects to the ha from the top) [10:05]
1. Go up and back. Mark the meem's point.
2. Leave "one and a half two and a half" nuqta between the meem's edge and the left side (the number is unclear; see below). This space is where the next move is dragged.

Move 2: into the ha [10:35]
1. Go downward and connect on the right side of the meem. Add 1.5 nuqta.
2. Drop 2 nuqta. Start curving and move downward, then stop.

Move 3: the ha [11:09]
1. Go back for the ha, all the way to the mark.
2. The ha is nearly straight but not quite: a slight rise then fall. Calligraphy avoids dead-straight lines.
3. The inner end of the ha is a move you can add at any time [12:11].

Move 4: second meem [12:41]
1. From the ha's end, mark 1 nuqta. Drop 2 nuqta: 1 for the end of the second meem and 1 for the dal (?).
2. Go down, curve back before the mark, and stop. The second guide nuqta shows where the meem stops.
3. Meem height 1 nuqta. The curve height is also 1 nuqta.

Move 5: dal [13:42]
1. The dal reaches 1 nuqta past the ha's edge and is the same height as the meem. It is a long way, so split it into two moves.
2. First, from the meem, move back and out with a curve to the right. Going left, the stroke gets thicker [14:44].
3. The meem stroke should flow naturally into the dal. Around the join, reverse the curve for the dal.
4. Then the dal proper: 2 nuqta from that point and 4 nuqta long, using the normal dal ending, which curves back to connect.

Practice [18:22]: watch the dal's top curve. Keep a gap between the ha and the dal. Drill "meem + dal" together, then "ha + meem", to learn where the letters sit.

### Warnings / traps
- Allah's lams are 3 nuqta, shorter than the 7-nuqta alif. The second lam is shorter than the first.
- Allah's ha needs a sharp "nose" at the turn, not a round curve, and is lighter than full thickness.
- The teacher has "never seen" Allah written another way in this script. A version with a normal ha exists, but this is the famous one.

Unclear:
- [2:06] "long nebulard" / "legendary": the reference shape and the earlier "half a point" inclination are garbled.
- [3:08] "more like the dial one rather than the or the neberal one": dal head vs nun (?).
- [5:25] Whether the second lam is "a bit higher" or shorter is contradictory. The transcript says both "stop shorter than the first lam" and "this would be a bit higher".
- [7:30]-[8:32] Ha turn points and the "higher point" left are board-dependent.
- [10:05] "one and a half two and a half points": either 1.5 or 2.5, or 1.5–2.5. Not certain.
- [12:41] "one point to Mark the let's say the the": what the second nuqta marks is not stated (probably the dal or the curve).

## Lesson 33: Writing Thuluth Script 1 (method for a plain line of text: draft, baselines, word joins, then marks and fillers)

Text used: the hadith "min husni islami al-mar'i tarkuhu ma la ya'nihi" ("part of a person's good Islam is leaving what does not concern him"). The draft becomes two lines, with "hadith sharif" added at the end.

### Order of work (the method) [0:00], summary [30:44]
1. Choose the sentence first.
2. Rule a straight pencil baseline. This is the main line.
3. Sketch the letters in pencil (the teacher uses a ballpoint so the camera can see it). This is where you choose among the many alternate forms and combinations.
4. Sketch at the scale of the real pen. Here that is a 3 mm pen: every sketched movement should be the size a 3 mm nib would make [2:10].
5. Add guide lines for heights and drops as you go, by measuring (e.g. "three and a half centimeters") [6:22].
6. Write the draft with the real pen over the sketch. Do not follow the outline slavishly; adjust as problems appear [12:37].
7. Order on the draft: letters first, then dots, then harakat [16:38], then decorations [17:41].
8. The finished draft is a blueprint. Scale it to any size or material. Trace it with tracing paper or a light table: "no shame", "people have been doing this for ages" [37:00].
9. If a letter in the draft is not good enough, write it again on scrap paper with a proper nuqta scale and use that version in the final piece [29:43].

### Word 1: "min" (meem + noon) [2:41]
1. Meem: the form that goes up and down, stops, and comes back.
2. Noon [3:14]: go down, then the noon's cup sized for the 3 mm pen.
3. The maximum drop for a small noon is 2 nuqta. The noon is 5 or 6 nuqta wide (the teacher says both).
4. The noon ends 1 nuqta lower, and the next word sits on that end. The words touch.

### Word 2: "husni" (ha + sin + noon) [4:16]
1. The ha's back stroke must touch the end of the previous noon. So start the ha knowing it goes down, up and back.
2. The ha drops 1 nuqta and is 5 nuqta wide [4:47].
3. Draw an inclined guide for the ha, running down to the sin and on to the noon.
4. The guide line from the noon of "min" continues across the whole length. Every later noon drops to the same line and comes back.
5. Add the noon's dot.

### Word 3: "islami" (alif with hamza below + sin + Lam-Alef + meem) [5:50]
1. The alif can start inside the previous noon's bowl, after its dot. The hamza goes below the alif.
2. The sin sits on the end of that noon and touches it.
3. The Lam-Alef goes down, breaks, and comes back [7:26].
4. Meem: the elongated (dropping) form, about 2 nuqta, ending on the word's own line [7:26].

### Each word has its own line [7:26]
- There is a main line, but each word also has its own line, and the words sit together on the main line.
- Example word (garbled, possibly "kafir"/"kafur" (?), with a waw): its path rises slightly, so the word is slightly inclined [7:56].
- All alifs and Lam-Alefs reach the same height [8:26].

### Word 4: "al-mar'i" [8:26]
1. Use the lam connected to the meem. Leave a good distance.
2. Ra: goes back, then up and around. The ra ends at the same drop as the meem, "because they are the same drop".
3. The hamza can go at the back or be left there (?).

### Line 2: "tarkuhu ma la ya'nihi" [9:30]
1. The text becomes two lines. Draw a boundary, then drop a little more for the second line.
2. Normal ta. Normal ra "with two coins" (?). For the kaf, a curve that may connect to the next letter. It came out bigger than expected [10:02].
3. Back, up, down, and connect to a meem, "the same concept as up here" (same as "al-mar'i") [11:03].
4. Use the alif option that gives "a nice intersection" here. Then the noon [11:03].
5. Add "hadith sharif" at the end to fill the short line [11:35].

### Writing the draft with the pen [12:07]
- Any change is made now, on the draft.
- Even for a big final pen, design at the smaller size: it is easier and faster.
- One fix made: a letter needed a bigger area, and that changes the whole script. The sin should go a bit lower ("one and a half sin" (?)). The shifting noon forced the dimensions to be re-evaluated [12:37]-[13:07].
- Keep every letter rule from earlier lessons in mind while writing [13:43].

### Harakat and decoration on the draft [17:41]
- Marks should not stick out beyond the boundary lines of the text [18:12]. The outlines exist to set the bounds.
- Over the hamza, put the kasra directly beneath it [18:43].
- A full-thickness (same pen) fatha adds weight. If you use it, it must be the same thickness as the writing [19:13].
- Fill open spaces with fillers (mizan, leaves), but do not overdo it. Letters are the main attraction; marks are delicate [20:48].
- Place harakat over the area their letter occupies. Overlap is acceptable: noon over ha, and there is still room for the kasra [22:18].
- Put a damma lower than a neighbouring mark so it clearly belongs to its letter [24:22].
- Choose a mark's form by fit. Here the small sukun, because the other would be too big [23:21].
- Madd can fill a gap where a madd is due [24:53].
- Use a disambiguation sign (ha, ain) above or below to fill a gap. A mizan can be placed beneath to "hold" a mark [24:22], [26:26].
- Do not let marks rise above the Lam-Alef [26:26].
- In jali/tarkib (next lesson) letters are packed tighter and placing marks is harder [22:50].

### Composition rules [30:13]-[36:30]
1. Curved baseline [31:16]: bend the main line gently, not sharply. Each word sits on the curve as a tangent and keeps its own line. Each word is a "box" placed on the general line.
2. Plain writing has few decorations.
3. Some intersections are fine, but don't overdo it. Keep it clear and simple.
4. Touch letter ends between words whenever you can. The small touches make the text flow from word to word [33:22].
5. Choose letter forms to fill space. For example, pick the ra/variant that fills the gap under two letters rather than one that leaves a hole; the "solid, stable" ra; a kaf that fills the gap above (it shrinks the text but gives a better shape) [33:53].
6. Pick the meem variant that makes a touch and a link [34:25].
7. Balance the weight. One area had too many intersections (two with the Lam-Alef) and was heavier than the rest. Fix it by swapping a form, e.g. a normal meem in place of the dropping meem, to open space [34:57].
8. Balance the descents. Use an ain that drops down and back, or a ha drop, so the gaps between descents are about equal. Mix drops and "up" shapes evenly; avoid crowding two congested areas together [35:29]-[36:30].
9. Any variant is allowed "as long as it looks nice and is following the rules".

### Warnings / traps
- Do not sketch at a random size. Sketch at the movement size of the real pen.
- The draft is not the final piece. Expect to rewrite letters on a scaled sheet.
- Decorations stay minimal. In real pieces they are fewer and finer than in the demo [38:35].

Unclear:
- [3:14] "going down one point up two points down on one halfway" (the noon/meem guide numbers) is garbled.
- [7:56] The example word for "each word has its own line" is garbled ("kefir"... with a waw).
- [9:30] Hamza placement in "al-mar'i" ("put it out back here or let it") is unclear.
- [10:02] "a normal Rock with two coins" is garbled.
- [13:07] "one and a half scene" is unclear.
- [16:07]-[17:10] Much of the draft writing is silent or "[Music]". The visible steps cannot be described.
- [18:12]-[26:26] Each mark placement is pointed at on the board ("here", "this area").

## Lesson 34: Writing Thuluth Script 2 (jali / tarkib: composing a phrase inside a shape)

Text used: a phrase translated as "love is an act of generosity". From the letters named (ha+ba, meem+noon, sheen with three dots, alif-lam, kaf, ra, meem) it is probably "al-hubbu min shiyami al-kiram" (?). The target shape is a circle.

### What tarkib (jali) is for [0:32]
- An artistic rendering of the text: intricate yet simple, intriguing, with interlocking words.
- It must stay readable. It shouldn't be readable at a glance, but it shouldn't be too hard either.
- Model to copy: the circular "al-Qur'an al-Karim" on Quran covers [1:33]. The alifs radiate round the circle (the teacher counts four alif/lam uprights). The noon continues the circle's edge, and "al-Karim" brings the circle back round, closed by the ra and the meem. The teacher recalls this from memory and is unsure of the details.

### Three basic requirements [3:06]
1. A shape (here a circle).
2. A script (Thuluth).
3. A balance between artistry and readability. Not too complicated, but not dull or unbalanced.

### Step order
1. Choose the sentence [3:39].
2. Write it out plainly in Thuluth with a normal pen [5:14]. This shows which letters you have and what combinations are possible, so you don't forget a prettier alternate. The proportions only need to be close.
3. Sort the letters into three zones [6:45]:
   - High letters (alif, lam, kaf) go above the main line. They fill the shape's height and long areas.
   - Middle letters sit on the main line.
   - Descending letters go below the line. Use their endings to balance the bottom of the shape.
4. Fix the reading order (see below).
5. Choose the shape [13:31]. Letter forms are chosen to fill the shape.
6. Draft many arrangements in pen. Leave it, come back the next day, and repeat until happy [26:35], [33:32].
7. Trace the chosen draft onto fresh paper inside the shape, refining it [35:06]. All calligraphers do this.
8. Then add the dots. They may be moved below if they don't fit where they belong [39:50].
9. Then the harakat, with a third-width pen, in the same ink colour as the letters [40:21].
10. Then leaves and mizan in the remaining gaps, especially under the harakat to "hold" them [41:25]. Use the minimum.

### Fill with letters, not decorations [7:46], [42:58]
- The shape is filled by the letters and their thickness, balanced against the negative space. Decorations and harakat do not do the filling.
- "Use the letters to fill in the gaps, do not use the decorations." Don't force a letter into place.

### Reading order [8:16]
- Never reorder words to make them fit (e.g. "Karim" before "Qur'an"). It confuses the reader and the piece loses its integrity.
- The simplest order is a straight line, right to left. You can also stack the text in levels, reading upward or downward [9:22]-[10:57]. A vertical top-to-bottom arrangement is possible.
- If a reader can jump from one word to a lower one and read the words in the wrong order, the layout is wrong [12:31].
- Trap [9:55]: if a letter touches another word, the eye follows it. Make sure the touch leads to the next word, not a dead end.

### The word "Allah" in compositions [10:57]
- "Allah" (lafz al-jalala) is never placed low. It goes top centre. If that is impossible, put it top right; failing that, top left.
- In Quran verses: Allah sits at the top (sometimes larger and clearer), and the rest of the verse goes below.

### Allowed shapes [13:31]-[17:11]
- Common: circle, rectangle, ellipse (vertical or horizontal), half circle, the "hazelnut" (wide base with a tip at the top), the half ellipse / dome.
- Avoid irregular shapes. The diamond is "usually done with" another style. Squares are "not much" used. The teacher has never seen a triangle.
- Example brief: a wedding card with the phrase in a central circle and the two names to the right and left in Diwani jali.
- Start small: two words (your name, family or friends' names), then three, then four.

### The worked draft [17:42]
1. Start "al-hubb" at the far right, as in the model.
2. Draft 1: a ha drawn one way and closed left a massive space below. Filling it with "min" would push "min" above the line and confuse the reader. Keep each word inside its own area [18:15].
3. Use the alternate ha form that extends to fill the area on the right. The ba then runs along and fills the space, which secures the reading of the first word [18:46].
4. Meem and noon fill the next space. This created an intersection [19:17].
5. Trap [19:50], [24:32]: the end of the ba met the noon's turn in one spot. Two thick changes of direction jammed together are ugly and confusing. The reader can't tell whether they connect or a letter is hidden behind. Typical spacing is a one-nuqta or two-nuqta gap, depending on the letters.
6. Sheen goes where its three dots can fill a space. Al- above. The kaf must be made smaller. Then ra, and a long meem (up then down) where a short one would confuse [20:24].
7. Balance check [20:54]: two meem shapes that pair well, a noon that rotates to close that part of the circle, two alif-lam uprights with the sheen's three dots between them, and the kaf balancing its side of the circle. A thick fatha fills one space [21:25].
8. While writing with the real pen: leave 2 nuqta between the ha and the next letter, because the ha goes down, left, then back [22:56].
9. Fixes: move "al-" left and put its alif further out so it closes the shape. Shorten the lam to control the ha [23:29].
10. Draft 2 [26:03]: drop the meem-noon and let the dots fill its gap. A meem going down fills the space between noon and the next letter. Pull al-kiram down under the sheen. A second meem fills another gap.
11. Draft 3 refinements [30:27]: change a meem to a form that goes the other way. Move "min" (meem-noon) into the space under the ba. Re-scale the ha (it was off scale, which also widened it). The result is a flowing chain: meem curve into noon into the sheen's meem.
12. Alternative idea [32:29]: two circles linked by a long stretched sheen, with the three dots placed at the link.
13. Final traced version [35:37]: al-hubb with the ba connected back. Meem-noon up top, curving to close the circle (it became an ellipse). The ba's dot can go "here or at min". al-kiram: up with the kaf, then ra. Sheen-meem fitted in. The sheen's three dots stacked on top of each other where space is tight [40:21].
14. Picking a different meem made a smooth transition with the ba, instead of forcing one to balance over it. The ha and ba could also be dragged down and flattened to fill the area under the ha [43:28].

### Harakat and fillers in tarkib [40:21]
- Third-width pen, same colour as the letters.
- Place the marks into small gaps, e.g. above the kaf, and below for the meem's kasra.
- Then leaves and mizan only where gaps remain, and under harakat to support them. Prefer a mizan over a weaker filler where it fits [42:28].
- Ha/meem-style disambiguation signs can also fill gaps [42:28].
- Keep it minimal: "the main focus are the letters".

### Warnings / traps
- Don't rush. Design over several days and "sleep over it". Do not design and write a piece on the same day [22:56], [33:32].
- Every first choice of letter form is provisional. Expect to throw away drafts.
- Avoid word-order ambiguity above all else.
