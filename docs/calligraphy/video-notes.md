# Notes from single lesson videos

The user sends YouTube lesson videos to show how a letter is written. The
34-lesson course has its own notes in [thuluth-course.md](thuluth-course.md).
This file covers the single videos sent apart from the course. Where the
notes changed a letter, the ledger entry is linked.

The videos themselves are not kept in the repo. The subtitles and the
working notes are on the NAS, under
`Calligraphy resources/thuluth/video notes/`. As with everything else in
these docs, no teacher, channel or calligrapher is named.

## Meem (2026-10-23)

[Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3CafNcwJ38A). Notes from its
frames, at each place where the pen touches down.

The head is three strokes, with the pen lifted between them, as in the
studio's pieces:
1. **The arch.** It begins at the head's left with a short climb up and to
   the right. It goes over the top and comes down the right side at full
   width, to the head's bottom right, ending in a flat cut.
2. **The bottom band.** It starts under that cut and runs right to left,
   closing the counter.
3. **The tail.** A new stroke from the head's lower left.

The straight-tailed meem is written the same way: the arch from the left;
then the left side, down across the bar; then the bar, from the right,
running on into the tail in one stroke.

What it changed: the meem's method and the refits of 552 and 553b (see
the ledger's Meem section and "The refits from the video's order").

## Ya (2026-10-23)

[Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=at--bWvIykU).
1. **The head.** The pen lands under the right dot and climbs straight up
   half a nuqta, with the nib at about 40°. This makes a thick bulb whose
   sharp corner points down and to the left. Without lifting, the pen
   rolls over the top and sweeps down to the left into a hair.
2. **The S-curve.** The pen lifts after the head. It is set down again
   where the head ended, then goes down to the right, curving at both ends.
3. **The tail.** It starts at the S-curve's end and goes down to the left
   2.5 nuqta, then straight left, and up into the curve.

What it changed: the ya's head (a 40° climb, kept on 718, 719 and 720),
and the ya written as two strokes split at the lift (see the ledger's
"The ya's head in a lesson video" and "Two strokes that meet where the
pen lifts"). The user's ruling: "It doesn't have to match the studio. It
is fine if it matches the video."

## Overview of a Thuluth vector sheet (2026-10-23)

[Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S-BdXh1VvwM). This is a tour of a
commercial vector sheet, not a writing lesson. What bears on our work:
- **One piece is one pen stroke.** Every letter is broken down into its
  separate pen strokes, the hilya (the ornamental mark) included. This is why
  the studio's pieces can be read as strokes, and why two pieces that meet
  usually mean the pen lifted there. The ya's head and S-curve are two
  pieces, and the video above shows the lift.
- **Few anchor points.** The curves are drawn with as few points as
  possible, so a piece can be moved without losing its shape.
- **More than 500 letter variations**, taken from historical compositions.
  They are arranged as standalone, initial, middle and final forms, along
  with the traditional pair tables (the mashq).
- **New joins from old pieces.** A word can join pieces that never joined
  traditionally. In the video's example, the fa of one form was joined to
  a kaf whose top stroke was removed. This is the same idea as our stroke
  tarkib.
- **Most words are made of pairs.** Since most words are built from letter
  pairs, the pair tables make composing quick.
- **Tashkeel** comes as its own section and is placed after the letters.
- **A flat version** is also sold, with each letter in one piece instead of
  separate strokes, for simple uses.
