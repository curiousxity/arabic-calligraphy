Calligraphy Course / Diwani Jali", 14 lessons), taken 2026-10-23 at the user's request ("save all the notes from the

## What the course means for our strokes

The points that bear on splitting letters and joins into pen strokes, drawn together from the lessons below.

- **Letters are built from a few shared moves.** The alif's ending (a long stroke down, the pen turned so it thins)
  ends the dal, the waw, the ta and the long mim; the fa's head (two moves in a 1-by-1-point box) heads the waw and the
  qaf; the nun's cup ends the sin, sad, qaf and ya; the ba's opening starts the initial ain, the long sin and the
  connected dal. A stroke fitted once can be reused across letters.
- **Stroke counts and order for the letters we have approved:**
  - waw: three moves, a cup for the head, then back over the top closing it, then the tail from the head's right side
    (lesson 7, seen in the frames). Our h65-waw's green / blue / red.
  - dal: two moves, a short high hook and then the alif's ending (lesson 3). Our h65-dal's two pieces.
  - ra: three shapes; the 4-point one starts with a ba move, goes down then left and closes with the alif's ending
    (lesson 3).
  - initial ain: the body first (a ba-like flick up, then down into the next letter), the head second, starting on the
    body (lesson 6). Our h66-ala's jaw and head.
- **Heads and tops come last.** The ain's head, the ha's head (lessons 2, 6, 12), the ta's alif (lesson 5) and the
  kaf's cap (lesson 8) are separate strokes added after the body, often at the end of the word. In سحاب the stroke on top
  of the ha goes in after the whole word; for سبح the ha's top is its own stroke.
- **Ba before ha** becomes a thin loop on the pen's corner, and the ha starts again from the top (lesson 2). Ba before
  sin is the tall ba, 3 points (lesson 4).
- **Long endings are stopped and finished later** (lessons 12-14): ta, ba, nun and the boat's planks and oars are left
  unfinished while the next words go down, then completed. A long ending is a separate, later stroke starting where the
  letter stopped.
- **Two pens.** A second pen for heads, caps and harakat: a third of the main nib in lessons 1, 6 and 8, a quarter in
  lessons 11, 12 and 14 (or one pen used on part of its edge, lesson 1). Our fitter knows one nib; the isolated ain's
  head, kaf caps and the small marks will need a narrower one.
- **Turning the pen thins a stroke's end** (lessons 1, 3, 4): rotating it in the fingers lifts part of the nib, so the
  ink narrows smoothly. A tapering tail is one stroke with a turning nib, not a separate hairline.
- **Pen breaks are not design** (lesson 14): he lifts to move his hand or re-dip; a break in a long stroke is not
  necessarily a new stroke.

## Lesson 1: Alif and Ba (YouTube `NcXtO4Ia5pQ`)

### General points
- **Prerequisite.** The teacher tells beginners to watch the course's Ruq'a videos first (00:04–00:25).
- **Pen angle for alif (00:36–01:10).** The nib is held "slightly perpendicular" and shown on camera. The captions give no number of degrees.
- **Dot angle and shape (01:11–01:40).** The dot is made at a little more than 45°. Dots in this script are **square**, neither thin nor wide. They are the measuring unit ("points") for the whole script.
- **Arm movement (01:43–03:06).** Write from the elbow and shoulder, not the wrist, so a long stroke is one continuous move without stopping to shift the hand. Lift the hand off the paper while writing.
- **Two pens (07:25–07:56).** One is the full nib width. The other is one third of that width and is used for decorations and small heads. At the end (20:58–21:18) the teacher adds that you can get by with one pen if you learn to give full, half, third or quarter width by controlling how much of the nib touches the paper.
- **Pen rotation (06:29–07:09).** Rotate the pen around the middle finger. This lifts part of the nib off the paper, the ink follows the lifting edge, and you get a smoothly rounded, narrowing end.
  - Fallback (07:09–07:24): stop, draw the outline of the remaining shape, and fill it with the same ink.

### Alif (isolated)
**Proportions (03:09–05:05)**
- Height scale of **5 points**.
- Draw an imaginary vertical line on the right. The top of the alif starts **half a point** away from it, so the letter leans by half a point. The bottom is also half a point from that line (04:46–04:58).
- The alif is not straight. It has two curves, like passing between two balls set next to each other (03:50–04:06).
- The second part is described as **4 points going downward**. The captions do not make clear whether those 4 points overlap the 5-point scale or add to it.

**Moves**
1. **Upper part (04:12–04:33).** Start beside the half-point mark at the top. Travel down, curving one way and then back (at 06:18 he names the curves: first to the right, then to the left). Touch the guide line and stop partway down.
2. **Lower part (05:00–05:17).** Start from where move 1 stopped and continue down about 4 points. Near the end, rotate the pen to make the rounded, tapering finish.
   - Joint (05:19–06:02): move 2 wraps around the end of move 1. The **outer edge must be smooth**; the inner edge may keep a sharp angle.
   - Repeated without the scale at 06:16–06:29: down with a curve right, a curve left, move 2, rotate, finish.
3. **Decoration** on the top-left of the alif, made with the third-width pen (07:51–10:20). The captions call it "a helium a decoration", which is garbled; the actual term is not recoverable. Variants:
   - **Type A (08:14–08:48), four moves:**
     1. Go down.
     2. Go back up in a circle that touches the side of the alif.
     3. Come back with a double-curve move.
     4. Finish with the same move as the alif's ending.
   - **Type B (08:50–09:14):** a closed circle, then go up. The top is a sharp corner, not rounded. Go back and continue with the same moves.
   - **Type C (09:18–09:30):** stop partway and add one more stroke that closes the shape.
   - **Attachment rule (09:34–10:12):** the decoration's last stroke goes *into* the alif body and comes back out, so it merges with the alif and holds onto it ("to give it strength").
- **Summary (10:14–10:26):** the alif is three moves (upper part, lower part, decoration). The decoration style is free.

### Ba (isolated)
**Proportions (10:36–13:12)**
- He first says 7 points of height, corrects it to **6 points** (10:49–10:55), and later confirms 6 points of height (12:58).
- The width is variable. Some writers use 5, but he says to take **7** for now (11:54–12:15, 13:03–13:10).
- Hold the pen **somewhat more vertical than for the alif** (11:33–11:41).
- The shape is one curve, a transition, a second curve, and a round finish, counted "1, 2 (transition), 3" (10:58–11:14).
- A bottom guide line marks where the stroke hits before turning back (11:23–11:29).

**Moves (11:14–12:56)**
1. Start at the top: a short rise, then come back and curve across the top (11:14–11:23).
2. **Transition:** run nearly straight for a short way (12:21–12:44).
3. Keep going down to the guide point, then curve the other way (11:43–11:49).
4. Last move: curve back to finish (11:52–11:56).
- **Ending (12:47–12:56):** either close the end one way, or go back and add one more move. The captions do not describe either shape. Free choice.

**Dots (13:14–13:37)**
- Ba: one dot below. Ta and tha: dots above.
- Two and three dots are written as **separate square dots**, not the joined dot shapes of ordinary Diwani.

### Connections
- **Initial ba to alif, the ba-alif ligature (13:52–14:48):**
  1. Use the first move of the ba, but don't go all the way down; stop and curve upward into the alif.
  2. The rising alif is not straight. It bends slightly to the left, very little, but visibly (14:16–14:35).
  3. At the top, finish with a straight downward cut, which gives the decorative top (14:35–14:43).
  - Overall it is up–down at the start, then up into the alif.
- **Two dentals in a row (14:53–15:32):** up, down, up, down. The second tooth rises because the connection comes from below. Example word: probably *bathth* (بث), which the teacher glosses as "broadcast" (15:28–15:32).
- **Three or more dentals (15:35–17:09):** these must not look like a sin, so vary the tooth heights.
  - First tooth low, second high.
  - Third and fourth are free; both can be low, because in his example the fifth goes high.
  - Dot placement is free.
  - The example word is garbled ("death beat"). It is described as long with many teeth. My guess is *tathbīt* (تثبيت), five dentals, but that is a reconstruction.
  - These raised and lowered teeth are new shapes used when dentals repeat (16:58–17:09).
- **Laith (ليث), "lion", also a boy's name (17:11–18:33):**
  1. The lam is written like an alif (17:22–17:27).
  2. At the bottom it turns back and stops; that turn is the ya (17:27–17:33).
  3. Come back and write the tha (17:33–17:37).
  4. Add the top decoration (17:40–17:44).
  - Detail (18:03–18:30): turn back with a small curve at the top. At the next connection make a slight upward curve, then write the final ba-shape body, described as a crisp curve followed by a sag.

### Warnings and common mistakes
- Changing from the ba's upper curve to its lower curve without the short straight transition gives an ugly ba (12:24–12:34).
- At the alif's joint, the outer contour must be smooth (05:44–06:00).
- Wrist-only writing forces stops and breaks straight lines (01:57–03:00).
- Equal-height teeth in a row turn into a sin (15:40–16:39).
- Writing a dot before the decoration leaves too little room for the decoration (19:46–19:53).

### Practice words
- **bathth (بث), "broadcast"** (15:12–15:32): up–down, up–down.
- **tathbīt? (garbled)** (15:57–17:09): first tooth low, second high, third and fourth free, fifth high.
- **Laith (ليث), "lion"** (17:11–18:33): lam as alif, turn back into the ya, connection with a slight upward curve, final tha with a crisp curve then a sag, then the decoration.
- **Word "to prove something"** (18:46–20:38). The captions are garbled ("it to prove something"). It is probably *ithbāt* (إثبات), my reconstruction from the letters shown.
  1. Alif: down, curve right, curve left, second move with the pen rotated (18:53–19:10). Top decoration left for later.
  2. Dental shapes for the middle letters (19:18–19:30).
  3. Second alif: down, up, a slight lean left, and a drop back at the top (19:30–19:41).
  4. The decoration, which he notes should have come before the dot (19:46–19:53).
  5. Final ta: curve and back, with a pause before the last move (20:01–20:11). Its dots can go inside or outside (20:11–20:16).
  - Hamza is postponed to a later lesson (20:38).


## Lesson 2: Jim, Ha, Kha (YouTube `-t3uzhCIGw8`)

### General points
- **One shape, three letters (00:05–00:12, 06:44–06:56).** Jim, ha and kha are identical except for the dot.
  - Jim: a dot below or inside the bowl; the captions just say "here or here".
  - Kha: a dot above, at one of two positions.
- **Pen angle (00:20–00:31).** Slanted, the same as for the alif.
- **Guide dots (00:12–00:27).** Put 4 points side by side at the top to fix the width of the **head**. The **body is 5 points wide** (01:10–01:23, 01:43–01:47).
- **Pens (04:26–04:37, 06:37–06:44).** The "head" stroke (move 4) is made with the third-width pen. This also avoids putting down too much ink.
- **Plan the head width first (12:39–12:56).** Laying out the 4 points before starting keeps the head from coming out too small and from crowding a neighbouring alif.

### Ha (isolated), body type 1: closed body
**Proportions (02:07–02:17, 03:13–03:24)**
- Body 5 points wide.
- About 1 point of drop below the line and about 1 point of rise above it. He compares this to some earlier letter, but the word is garbled ("philip").
- The one-point spaces on the two sides are not equal; the right one is larger.
- About **2 points of height between the tail and the head**.

**Moves**
1. **Top stroke (00:36–01:38).** A two-curve move: down and to the left with a curve, then back to the right. The head spans the 4 guide points.
2. **Body, going down (02:17–02:38).** Start from the same point, in the same way as the alif. The starting angle looks different from the top stroke. The first (lower) curve is roughly symmetric and easy.
3. **Body, coming back up (02:38–02:55).** **Turn the pen as you go up to the right**, so this curve is smoother and rises higher than the first, even though both reach the same height.
4. **Tail (02:55–03:13).** From the top, go back with the tail: reverse, curve the other way, come back, and **rotate the pen** at the end. This ending rises upward.
5. **Head detail, third-width pen (04:26–05:41).**
   1. Return to the top at the 4 points.
   2. Start with a hairline that joins the body seamlessly and runs smoothly downward.
   3. Rise in a curve, touch the top, and come back at **full thickness** (one curve one way, one curve the other).
   - Alternative ending: curve down and finish in a small circle (05:27–05:37). Other head shapes are acceptable too.
   - The teacher numbers the body moves as "first, second and third" and this head stroke as "the fourth move" (04:24–04:29).

**Thuluth-like variant (03:24–04:12)**
- Normal head; go down; come back with the wider curve to the right.
- When you return, **straighten the pen** and finish in a shape that goes **down**, about 1 point, instead of up.
- **When to use which (04:12–04:24):** both are correct. The downward ending suits a continuation that has to go down; the upward ending suits one that goes up.

### Ha, body type 2: open, mim-like (05:43–06:44)
1. Go down, but **do not close the body**. Stop.
2. Add two curves: the first follows the bowl of the ha, and the second swings outward and back.
   - The arrow on screen is only a direction marker and is not part of the letter.
   - The teacher sometimes adds small personal swerves here, like a grape vine (06:15–06:30).
3. Return to the top and do the head stroke (move 5 above), ideally with the smaller pen.

### Connections
- **Jim and ha join from the top, never from the bottom (06:56–07:08).** Two ways:
  - **(a) Diwani style (07:08–07:29):** the previous letter comes down, stops, and the head is written at full thickness, as in ordinary Diwani.
  - **(b) Hairline (07:29–07:47):** the thin trailing tip of the previous letter becomes the hairline start of the head.
- **Ba + ha (08:05–09:23):**
  - **Option 1 (08:09–09:04):**
    1. Rise as for a ba, then come down like a small ba.
    2. Lift the pen onto its edge so that only the tip writes. The ba thins into a hairline that imitates the ha's head stroke. Up, down, rotate: a figure-eight ("infinity") motion.
    3. Stop, come back from below, and continue with the normal ha moves.
  - **Option 2, simpler (09:04–09:23):** up and down with one curve rising all the way, without the double curve. Then simply turn back and finish halfway with the ha head shape.
- **Alif or lam + ha (09:28–10:12):** the bottom of the lam turns into the hairline, followed by a normal ha, then the decoration. Or stop and use a plain Diwani ha. He usually prefers the hairline version but sees uses for both. He mentions *al-ḥamdu* as an application (10:08–10:12).
- **The shape before the next letter (10:17–12:14).** The ha's form is chosen by the letter that *follows* it, not the one before (11:58–12:04).
  - **Next letter joins from above** (he names mim, ha/jim and ra; the rest of his list is garbled): the ha must curve **down then up**, so the next letter can attach from the top (11:00–11:45). Example: a jim going down into a following ha (11:19–11:32).
  - **Any other next letter** (alif and the rest): up, down, up (11:45–11:58).
  - So there are two shapes: up-then-down, and down-then-up (12:04–12:14).

### Warnings and common mistakes
- Without the 4 guide points the head comes out too small, or an alif placed before it ends up touching the head (12:44–12:56, 13:43–13:51).
- The head should curve downward a little more than in his first attempt (13:32–13:38).
- Writing while talking lowers quality; focus (14:26–14:31).
- Simply going up and turning straight into a following ra "isn't pretty". That point is made in Lesson 3 (13:14–13:22) and is listed there.

### Practice words
- **ḥājib (حاجب), "eyebrow"** (12:33–14:55). Near the end the captions also call it "hijab"; the letters shown are ha, alif, jim, ba.
  1. Lay out the 4 points.
  2. Ha top stroke: start at the alif angle, up, then down (12:56–13:03).
  3. Curve the other way so the alif can stand clear of the head (13:10–13:38).
  4. Ha head.
  5. Alif, written to **intersect** the ha, as practice for overlapping letters (13:53–14:00).
  6. Jim: stop and add its head (14:09–14:14).
  7. Ba: down, curve, back (14:40–14:48).
  8. Dots for jim and ba (14:48–14:53).
- **yaḥjub? (يحجب)** (15:13–16:55). The word itself is garbled; the captions name the letters ya, ha, jim, ba (16:46–16:55). Written with a smaller pen.
  - **Planning (15:23–15:56):** ya before ha takes one connecting shape. Ha before jim takes the other, because jim joins from above.
  - **Writing:**
    1. Start with the double-curve shape (16:01–16:06).
    2. Stop and immediately switch to the second shape (16:12–16:24).
    3. Jim, enlarged for balance (16:28–16:38).
    4. Ba, made smaller because the word is long (16:38–16:46).
  - **Space-filling version (16:56–18:32).** Ha body type 1 at full size, used to fill the gap under the jim.
    1. Ya runs down into the full-size ha and goes up.
    2. Bring the next stroke down low enough to enclose the jim (17:24–18:01).
    - He calls filling spaces with the letters themselves the essence of Diwani (18:06–18:12).
    - Recap (18:21–18:32): come back into the ha, stop, go down, **rotate to thin**, come back, write the jim.
- **Ha followed by jim (another form of the same word)** (18:44–19:53). The captions say "two has hijab". Start with a normal ha into the jim. The jim can be thick or thin. He prefers this form because it is livelier.
  - Alternative (19:31–19:50): down, back up, come back, rise, ha head, ba below.
- **Diwani-style jim inside Diwani Jali (19:56–20:27):** stop and write an ordinary Diwani jim with a small nudge at the bottom. Acceptable, but he keeps it for tight space or a compact look.


## Lesson 3: Dal and Ra (YouTube `lfZlTRVN77M`)

### General points
- **Letter pairs (00:05–00:19).** Dal and dhal share one shape; ra and zay share another. Dhal and zay differ only by their dot.
- **Pen angle.**
  - Dal: the same as the alif, shown against a vertical line (01:46–01:55).
  - Ra shape 2: described as "very shallow", with the viewer referred to the footage (05:13–05:21).

### Dal (isolated)
**Proportions (00:23–00:47)**
- 2 points across for horizontal travel and 3.5 points of height.
- The first point on the right governs move 1 (00:52–00:57).

**How it differs from ordinary Diwani (00:57–01:15).** Ordinary Diwani writes the dal in three moves. Jali Diwani writes it in two: a curve, then a stroke going down.

**Moves**
1. **Short hook (01:18–02:00).** Start a little below the first point. It is a very high, short curve, short compared with move 2. Go up, then come back, rotating the pen. The turn at the top is "quite sharp" (03:04–03:09).
2. **Body (02:03–02:36).** This is the **alif's second move** (its rotated ending). It reaches 2 points from the guide line.
   - Go down, leaving 1 point of space to control the drop, then curve and finish.
   - It follows move 1 immediately (02:39–02:53).
   - **Rotation (03:16–03:33):** start rotating toward the end of the first part of the stroke, not just at the tip, to get the gradual thinning. The captions don't make clear exactly where this begins.
- The isolated dal sits with about 1 point above (03:36–03:40); the captions are vague here.

**Connected dal (03:41–03:58).** Rise like a ba, then come back with the dal body. It is demonstrated in the practice words.

### Ra / Zay: three shapes
**Shape 1: 4 points high, close to a ba (04:04–04:55)**
1. Start with a ba move and stop.
2. At about 45°, place a guard dot. It forces you to go **down first** and only then left, so the stroke doesn't drift left too early (04:21–04:41).
3. Go up, then down, then turn left (04:41–04:49).
4. Close with the **alif's ending move** (04:49–04:53).

**Shape 2: 2.5 points high, like Naskh and Thuluth (05:00–06:55)**
1. Go up, then down.
2. At the bottom, **start rotating the pen before the stroke turns upward**. The line visibly loses width, and the letter closes before it rises (05:27–05:44, 06:00–06:08).
- He compares it to the ba's move (06:08–06:15).
- **Mistake (06:18–06:41):** rotating too late gives a wide, blunt end. That is wrong.
- **Correct (06:41–06:55):** rotate early, so the thickness fades before the end and the change in thickness is smooth.
- **Use (06:56–07:07):** among connected letters where space is tight.

**Shape 3: starts with a 2-point stroke (07:08–07:39)**
1. A short two-curve stroke, like an alif compressed to 2 points.
2. A Diwani-style body that **closes downward instead of upward**.
- **With sin (07:39–08:20).** This shape often appears with sin. Sin is sometimes written in a long stretched form; the captions' word here is garbled ("cheetah"). That gives a very thin letter, after which you stop and turn back. A dal or ra can connect there, but only rarely with sin and shin.

### Connections
- **Ba + ra (08:20–08:52).** The ba joins **straight into** the ra, which simply goes **down**. That downward stroke is where the ra begins.
- **Dal versus ra (08:52–09:17, 10:14–10:22).** After a connecting letter, the dal must **go up** (a tooth rises, as in the ba) and then come back. The ra goes straight down. This is the key difference: get it wrong and you have written the other letter.
- **Ha/kha + ra (12:24–13:30).**
  - Using the big space-filling ha from Lesson 2, the ra can curve straight off the ha. He says it works and looks "kind of" good.
  - Going up from the ha and then turning straight into a ra "isn't pretty". The normal ra shape is the standard choice.

### Warnings and common mistakes
- In ra shape 2, rotating the pen late leaves a wide, blunt end (06:18–06:41).
- After a connecting letter, a dal without its upward rise reads as a ra, and a ra that rises reads as a dal (09:06–09:17).
- In ra shape 1, without the guard dot the stroke drifts left too early (04:34–04:41).

### Practice words
The words for "limit" and "heat" are not spelled out in the captions; the teacher gives only the meanings. Reading them as *ḥadd* and *ḥarr* is my inference from those meanings and from the letters shown.
- **ḥadd (حد), "limit"** (09:20–09:53)
  1. 4 points for the ha head.
  2. Ha in the form whose exit goes **up**, because the dal needs to rise.
  3. Add the dal.
- **ḥarr (حر), "heat"** (09:25–10:10): ha, then immediately straight down into the ra.
- **"Protector" word** (10:26–11:43). The caption reads "vatid", glossed as someone who protects territories. From the letters shown it is probably *rā'id* (رائد), my reconstruction.
  1. Ra in shape 1: up, down, left with pen rotation, closing at the bottom (10:50–11:06).
  2. Alif: down, left, and finish (11:08–11:21).
  3. Top decoration (11:21–11:26).
  4. A tooth like a ba, probably the hamza seat (11:28–11:33).
  5. Dal, connected as taught (11:33–11:38).
  - The hamza itself is postponed to later in the course (11:38–11:43).
- **zākhir? (زاخر)** (11:46–13:46). The caption reads "zacher" and the teacher says it relates to increase or abundance, roughly "teeming"; my reading. The letters shown are zay, alif, kha, ra.
  1. Zay in shape 2: up, then down 2.5 points, rotating straight away at the bottom (11:51–12:06).
  2. Alif brought down **inside** the zay so the two intersect (12:08–12:17).
  3. Decoration (12:17–12:24).
  4. Zay dot (12:24–12:30).
  5. Kha: either a normal one, or the big space-filling ha from Lesson 2 with the ra curving straight out of it (12:30–13:00).
  - The ra can also be written in its normal shape (13:22–13:30).
- **Closing recap (13:48–14:17).** Ra/zay usually connects going down. Its three shapes are: the 4-point one, the 2.5-point one, and the one that starts with a 2-point stroke and then goes down in Diwani style.

## Lesson 4: Sin and Shin (YouTube Ox7YniwjnKM)

### General points
- Sin has three teeth (nabras). When the letter stands alone, the third "tooth" is really the start of a nun cup, so an isolated sin is two teeth plus a nun cup.
- The teeth are written with the pen held close to vertical, and they lean. They are not upright strokes; they slant like the whole script.
- The cup leans left, the same way the Jali Diwani alif leans left of true vertical (02:09-02:31).
- Pens: the large pen for the letter forms. At 09:08 he switches to "the smaller pen" for the practice words, to fit more words on the page. The captions do not give its size in this lesson; Lesson 6 says the small pen is a third as wide.
- He describes three sins in all: two small ones and one long one.

### Small sin, shape 1 ("stop and come back")
Proportions (00:29-01:04):
- 1 point from the first tooth to the second.
- 1.5 points from the second tooth to the third. The gaps grow from right to left.
- Cup: 2 points. The dot drop inside the cup gives the same depth (03:10-03:19).

Moves:
1. **First tooth** (01:13-01:21). Pen near vertical. Go down along a slant and stop. One measuring dot fits inside this move.
2. **Second tooth** (01:21-01:46). It starts lower than the top of the first tooth, so the teeth step downward. Go down, then come back up. 1.5 dots fit inside this move. This creates a small dip below the first tooth.
3. **Into the cup** (01:48-01:58). Start the nun: drag down about half a point, far enough that the sin reads clearly.
4. **Cup** (02:33-02:49). Go down, then curve back up to the left, like the wisam (the ra/nun tail from earlier lessons). The two sides of the cup must be equal height. He says this is unlike other scripts.
5. **Finish** (03:30-03:42). Travel left and upward and end on the thin edge of the pen. At 03:38 his hand slips and he says the end must be thin, not thick.

- Where it ends: normally the cup's tip stops below the start of the sin (02:53-02:58). It can instead be given a decorative stop halfway up, like a nun (03:01-03:09).
- Written without the scale (03:19-03:42): tooth 1, tooth 2, tooth 3, back down, left, up, thin finish.

### Small sin, shape 2 ("fluid")
Shown 03:44-04:35.
1. **First tooth.** Go down with a very small nudge to the left, which gives the stroke its thickness. Then flick suddenly upward. The tooth is angled and points downward.
2. **Next tooth.** Leave almost one point. Go down and curve back up.
3. **Cup.** The same cup as shape 1.

- How the two shapes differ (04:35-04:57): shape 1 stops, comes back, and continues. Shape 2 runs on as one fluid line and comes out bigger. Keep the letter's slant in either case.
- When to use which (05:01-05:17): one or the other suits better in practice, and he says shape 2 is sometimes prettier with a certain neighbouring letter. The caption names that letter as "anta", which is garbled.

### Shin
Shown 05:18-05:49.
- Same body as sin, plus three dots above.
- Centre the dots over the middle tooth, a little to the right of centre, so the group sits over the letter.

### Long sin ("ten points wide")
Proportions (05:54-06:17):
- About 10 points wide. The counting at 06:03-06:13 is garbled ("we've got five and we need one more").
- 4 points high.
- It begins almost like a ba.

Moves:
1. **First move** (06:21-07:00). Start at the same angle as a ba. Go **up**, then **down**. The line thins on the way up, then thickens on the way down because of the pen angle. Then travel **left**. End with a gradual taper made by **rotating the pen in the fingers**, the technique from the ra lesson.
2. **Drop** (07:04-07:20). The drop is 2 points. Use the scale on the right side to place the dot that marks the sin's lower edge.
3. **Second part** (07:20-07:57). This is the same as the nun cup. It joins the first move about **in the middle** of it: not at the top, not at the bottom. He explains that the stroke is in thirds (a rising part, a falling part, and the middle) and the join sits in the middle third, the same place it joins on the normal sin.
4. **Cup** (07:57-08:13). 3 points wide, 1 point of drop inside. Go down, come back up, then make the ending shape.

- Use (08:20-08:56): he links this sin to a Jali Diwani layout he calls a "forman"/format that looks like a boat. The long sin fills a stretch with no other writing. This part of the captions is unclear.

### Connections
- **Ba before sin** (12:47-13:23). A ba before a sin must be the high ba, 3 points tall. Start at the top, go down, stop, then attach the sin directly to its left. Example: bismillah.
- **Letter after sin** (12:21-12:47). Plan around both neighbours. In sabt, the three teeth sit high, so the following ba drops low, and the final ta is written big.
- **Ha before sin** (13:24-13:47). Stop the ha, then start the sin from that point. Either small sin shape works. The join looks like an immediate stop followed by a fresh start, with the usual Diwani curvature.
- **Sin/shin before a letter that joins from above** (13:48-14:42). He gives two options:
  - Shape 1, which goes back: teeth 1, 2, 3.
  - Shape 2, which he calls easier: teeth 1, 2, 3, then go back.
  - Here he **rotates the pen to a more vertical angle**, because the stroke itself runs more vertically. This gives a clean thick-to-thin change; without it the shape can come out thick. He calls this personal preference.
- **Sin/shin at the end of a word** (14:58-15:12). It is joined from the right in the same way, with either small shape.
- **Long sin** (15:12-15:35). He advises against joining it from the right because it is so long. It is rarely used in joins at all, because it reaches 4 points down and leaves no room for following letters.

### Warnings
- Finish the cup on the thin edge, not thick (03:38).
- Keep the two sides of the cup equal (02:44-02:52).
- Watch the slant on every form, including the bigger shape 2 (04:59).

### Practice words (small pen, from 09:08)
- **sahab, "clouds"** (09:14-10:17). Caption says "ahab".
  - Sin in shape 2: down, sudden up, down.
  - Ha joined with the connection from the ha lesson, scaled 4 points with 1 point inside. The ha needs 4 points of scale.
  - Then the remaining letters. The captions read like "heart, then a nabra, then the bar".
  - The ba is carried up and closed on itself.
  - Scale for the sin: 1 point, then 1.5.
- **shadid, "strong"** (10:20-10:47). First small shin. The dal goes up, then all the way down. Then the teeth are counted 1-2-3, then a ya brought back. The order of the end of this word is unclear in the captions.
- **sharir, "evil"** (10:47-11:27). The same word with sin gives sarir, "bed".
  - The tooth used for the sin is the same one used to set up the ra.
  - Count three teeth for the shin, and the ra follows.
  - He calls this a review of the ra lesson.
  - A "four for the dal" count in the captions is unclear.
- **asir, "captive"** (11:31-12:16). This uses the long sin.
  - Alif, with its small decoration touching the next letter.
  - Long sin.
  - On the way back up, about half a point lifts the tail.
  - Then the ra from the last lesson, with a "touch" because the normal ra won't fit there.
- **sabt, "Saturday"** (12:21-12:47). Sin, ba low under the high teeth, big final ta.
- **bismillah** (12:51-13:23). High ba, 3 points, then the sin attached to its left.
- **hiss, "feeling"** (13:24-13:47). Ha, stop, sin.
- **sahab** again (14:03-14:23). Shown with each of the two join options.
- A word glossed as **"cheapness"** (14:43-14:56). Written with ha and shin, possibly shuhh. The captions are garbled.

### Review (15:38-16:14)
- The shape with 1.5 points in it (shape 1).
- The down-up-down-up shape (shape 2).
- A third shape: shape 1 with its end brought high up, like the ra variant.
- The shapes used with ha or mim, joining either way.


## Lesson 5: Sad, Dad, Ta, Za (YouTube Gz3FG1MLBiw)

### General points
- He treats all four letters as one family.
- Scale: 2 points laid horizontally side by side, plus 2 more for a second measurement.
  - The 2 horizontal points set how far the first stroke travels to the right.
  - 1 point sets the height of the loop (00:12-00:56).
- Pen held vertically at the start, which gives a thin line going upward (00:33-00:42).
- He switches to "a smaller pen" at 05:20 to write longer words.

### Sad (isolated)
Moves:
1. **Up and right** (00:56-01:08). Pen vertical. Go up first, so the thin part of the stroke is long enough, then go right as far as the 2-point boundary.
2. **Down and left** (01:10-02:13).
   - Stop at the right end. Place one point inside the loop, like an eye.
   - Curve down and travel left.
   - The gap between the loop and the tooth is 1 point.
   - On the way, the pen is naturally more slanted than at the start. **Do not resist that slant.** Its shallow angle at the start of the return gives the loop a rounded curve.
   - The finished loop should look **downward**, like an eye seen from above.
3. **Cup** (02:13-02:47). The same cup as the sin. Drag down to the half point, go down to 2.5, reach the midpoint, curve back up, and finish with a small decoration made with the tip of the pen.

- The letter looks tilted because the whole script is tilted (02:47-02:54).
- **Dad**: the same letter with one dot above (02:54-02:58).

### Ta and Za
Shown 02:58-04:17.
- Same 2-point and 2-point moves as sad, but the loop must be **shallower** than sad's. The caption's word for the look is "cheekier".
- Coming down from the loop is the same move as the end of an alif. The space inside is slightly smaller than in sad.
- The extension runs 2 points past the loop (the caption says "after the law"; the meaning is probably the loop).
- The second move is the same as the alif and ra ending.
- At the end, rotate the pen to fill in the lower edge.
- **Last, add the alif on top.** Two placements, which he calls personal preference or style:
  - Joined to the loop's edge, so the alif runs down and merges into the first move.
  - Standing in the middle of the loop.
- **Za**: add one dot.

### Connections
- **Before the family** (04:24-05:11).
  - A ba before these letters takes the high shape, as before a sin.
  - The sad or ta must start at least 1 point away from the previous letter.
  - **Rotate the pen** for the join, so the sad or ta loop runs smoothly into the line below.
  - The loop still points downward.
- **After the family** (05:11-05:17). Joining from the left works the same way. Don't forget sad's tooth.
- **Sad/dad before ha** (10:01-10:35). Ha has two shapes. Sad's tooth serves as the link into the ha. Ta has no tooth, so it must change into the other move to stay consistent. Don't confuse ta with sad.

### Variants
- **Tooth inside the loop** (08:57-09:20). For sad and dad, some writers put the tooth inside the loop. Example: sadid.
- **Rounded loops** (09:20-09:47). Some writers make a very rounded, smooth sad instead of a sharp one. Either is fine as long as the sads are uniform across a piece. He prefers sharp.

### Warnings
- Go up first in move 1 to get the thin part (01:03).
- Let the pen slant on the return; don't fight it (01:40-01:46).
- The loop must look downward (02:01-02:13).
- Ta is shallower than sad (03:09).
- Don't forget sad's tooth when you vary which letter gets the high tooth (06:37-06:52).

### Practice words (small pen, from 05:20)
- **basis, "a glimmer"** (05:26-06:32). The captions gloss it as "a very small amount" or "a hint of hope".
  - Ba going down. Carry the pen low so the sad joins smoothly.
  - Up for the sad, then back.
  - The join line runs into the line below. You can add ink to smooth it.
  - Stop, make the sad's tooth, then the ya. He changed the ya's shape here for variety.
  - Then continue.
- **High tooth on sad instead of ba** (06:37-06:52). As an alternative, the sad's tooth can be the high one instead of the ba.
- **tabib, "doctor"** (06:53-07:33).
  - Ta: go down, come back with the ba, add the alif for the ta.
  - Ba: go up, then down into a smooth ya.
  - Final ba.
  - Sad and ta differ by the tooth. Watch the tooth placement.
- A word glossed as **"to raise the voice / cry out"** (07:39-08:17). Probably yasdah.
  - Ya first, as a reminder that these letters need a high join before them.
  - Then sad, the sad's tooth, dal, and finally ha, as ha practice.
- **sad + ra** (08:17-08:50). The word sounds like "usor" or "you saw" in the captions.
  - With a following ra, either keep a single tooth for the sad, or just go up for the sad and come down.
  - The same applies to ta and za. For dad, add the dot.
  - This passage is partly garbled.
- **sadid** (09:10-09:20). The tooth-inside-the-loop variant.


## Lesson 6: Ain and Ghain (YouTube HHS_EqJ0tJ8)

### General points
- Scale: 5 points (00:13-00:39).
  - 3 points in a row set the upper side, which is the head.
  - Next to them, 2 points mark the limit of the lower side.
- The body is the same as the ha body. The ain adds a head, which starts 1 point away from where the body's first move begins (01:01-01:15).
- **Pen switch** (01:55-02:12): he changes to the small pen for the head. Jali Diwani uses two pens, one a third as wide as the other.

### Ain (isolated)
Overview (00:39-01:41):
- Move 1: down.
- Move 2: back. It has two shapes, and you can choose either.
- Move 3: the upper edge, which is the head.

Moves:
1. **Body, first move** (01:30-01:42). Go down in a smooth curve to the left, then start curving back.
2. **Body, second move.** Two options (01:42-01:55):
   - (a) Go down, **rotate the pen**, and come back.
   - (b) Do it exactly like the ha.
3. **Head, with the small pen** (02:12-02:27). Start from the edge, go up, then come back.
   - The head can be sharp, like the sharp ha, or smooth and circular (02:27-02:45).
   - The body continues from below the head.

Smaller variant of the body (02:45-04:05), useful because the full body can come out bigger than intended:
- Go left, then curve back. It doesn't need to be large.
- The other bottom: go down 1 point, then rotate the pen and go up 2 points. The right curve ends higher than the left.
- The return can either go upward or stay low, as in the ha lesson.

Proportions (03:47-04:03):
- The head starts 1 point from the edge and is 2 points.
- The body sits 2 points from the head.

### Ain (initial)
Shown 04:07-05:07.
1. Up and down, like the start of a ba. Stop.
2. Leave 1 point, then make the head.
3. Leave 1 point after the head, then go up into the tooth as usual.

- The drop is a little longer than a ba's, but it is the same kind of shape.
- **Leave real space under the back of the ain (the neck).** If it is shallow, the letter reads as a ha.

### Initial ain before the ha-shape letters
Shown 05:07-06:00.
- Ha has two curves. With certain letters (the caption says "these five letters") the join changes into the same move as the isolated body.
- Leave the 1 point at the top, then come back with that shape.
- The end of this move must be **thicker** than the other side.
- Shown joined to a mim and to a ha.

### Round head variant
Shown 06:00-06:30.
- A circular head, still started 1 point from the edge.
- Can take a ha after it.
- He compares it with the jim/ha (06:30-06:43): the ha and the ain share the same body.

### Body curvature warning
Shown 06:43-07:06.
- When the body goes down, turns left, and comes back, the turn is a **circular curve**, smooth, not a sharp stop.

### Ain (medial)
Three moves, coming in from a letter on the right that arrives at a downward angle. Example: after a jim (07:06-07:42).
1. **Up and to the left** (07:42-07:48). Stop.
2. **Back to the right** (07:48-07:54), with a curve at the top.
3. **Back and down** (07:54-08:02). This is the same third move as in the forms above:
   - If the next letter rises (joins from above), use the upward version.
   - If the ain is final, or joins into a ha, use the other version.

Examples:
- Ain + ba (08:27-08:41): up, stop, back, then the cup-like move into the ba.
- Ain + jim (08:41-08:58): the shape changes; one side of the ain is curved differently.

### Ain (final)
Shown 08:58-09:07.
- The same as the medial form, with the end shape added.

### Practice words
- **ajab, "something amazing or extraordinary"** (09:12-09:59).
  - The ain body joins a jim, so it goes down.
  - The jim joins onward, so it uses the rising shape.
  - **The ain's head is added afterwards**, then the final letter.
  - The captions sound like a Quranic phrase around 09:25, but it is garbled.
- **Arar, a city in northern Saudi Arabia** (10:01-10:37).
  - Ain, ra, ain, ra.
  - The second form must be bigger than the first.
  - Leave the 1 point, go back, then the final drop.
- **asir, "hard"** (10:37-11:36).
  - Initial ain: up then down.
  - Sin, then the small ra.
  - He first forgot a tooth. The word needs three teeth for the sin plus one for the ya.
- A medial ain shaped like the sin teeth (11:43-12:13). The passage is garbled: the captions read "you can actually use the shape of the sin... up and down... this is the nabra for the...". The word is unidentifiable.


## Lesson 7: Fa and Qaf (and Waw) (YouTube G_7Cc_q_vBA)

### General points
- He calls it a short lesson. Both letters share one head, and the new letters are built by combining moves from letters already learned (03:51-03:58).
- No pen change is mentioned in this lesson.

### Head (fa, qaf, waw)
Box: 1 point by 1 point (00:13-00:20).
1. **Down, then up** (00:20-00:41), as in ordinary Diwani. The **end of this move must be lower than its start**.
2. **Back** (00:38-01:05). Close the loop onto itself.

- Because the first move ends lower than it starts, the head looks **downward**.

### Waw
Shown 01:05-01:15.
- The fa head, then the ending of an alif.

### Fa (isolated)
Moves:
1-2. **Head** (01:15-01:18), as above.
3. **Body** (01:18-02:05). From the head, continue downward into a body like the ba but bean-shaped: start shallow, go down, then curve back up. Continued further, the curve would close into a bean.
4. **Ending** (02:05-03:18).
   - The body reaches about 4 to 4.5 points. A ba is 5.
   - He calls the ending the third or fourth move. Two options:
     - Go back with a **thick** ending.
     - A **thin** ending, like the Diwani ba ending.

- Demonstration sequence (02:53-03:18): down, left, up, then either ending.
- Dot: either joined to the end decoration or above the head.

### Qaf (isolated)
Shown 03:22-04:17.
1-2. **Head**: the same 1×1 head.
3. **Cup**: the nun cup, down then up. The backward move is the same as the nun's.
   - Proportions: 3 points inside, 2.5 points outside. The cup points slightly to the right.
4. **Ending**: rises and stops about midway up.

- Dots: above the ending or above the head.
- Jali Diwani does not use joined (connected) dots for qaf.

### Fa, alternative shape (his favourite)
Shown 04:17-06:31.
1. **First move** (04:23-04:31). Go down, like a flipped mim, for 1 point.
2. **Second move** (04:31-04:39). Go all the way back, almost 3 points.
   - Proportions vary between calligraphers. He gives height 2 points and width 3.
   - Some make the angle of this move shallow. He makes it **very sharp, rising steeply**. It is up to you.
3. **Body** (05:00-05:09). Down to the left, **rotate the pen**, come back.
4. **Ending** (05:44-05:49). Either stop, or add the thick ending. The dot sits naturally above.

- Character: much sharper in the body and cup than the regular fa, while the top stays smooth (05:09-05:19).
- Height: the height of this form governs something he calls the "arc piece". The captions are garbled.
- Joining: this form does join, but from a particular point he indicates on screen; the captions don't say where.
- Sequence recap (05:51-06:03): down, up, back, come back, dot.
- At first the start can come out thick and rectangular. With practice, make it curved (06:03-06:18).
- The important part must be **sharp, not curved** (06:18-06:31): either stop dead and go down, or make a very tight turn at the top.

### Fa/Qaf (medial)
Proportions: 1 point inside; on the outside, the up and rightward moves come to 2 points (07:32-07:41).
1. **Up** (07:11-07:15). Go down, then curve up, but not far.
2. **Back** (07:15-07:20).
3. **Out** (07:20-07:31). Go right and leave the letter.
   - The top is pointed like a cone, not round. Ordinary Diwani rounds it.
   - Four sharp corners: the left one sits very close to the baseline, the right one a bit higher (06:54-07:11).
   - The outgoing stroke is a different thickness from the return, and that difference shows the angle.

### Final forms
Shown 07:43-08:18.
- **Final fa**: the medial head with the bean body from the isolated fa.
- **Final qaf**: go down, then return to the isolated qaf's nun cup.
- The captions say the final qaf has a closed head while the fa's is open.

### Practice words
- **Drills** (08:18-08:26): fa-fa-qaf and fa-fa-ba.
- **faqat, "only"** (08:26-09:20).
  - Fa, go back, then qaf, then the final letter, which the caption calls "ba", i.e. the ta shape.
  - Height: "could be four points" (08:56-09:10). The caption doesn't say what this height refers to; it may be the fa written like an alif. It also depends on the main text height of the piece.
  - Keep the curve and the neck, and leave the space under the neck (09:13-09:20).
- **farid** (09:20-09:53). Written with the alternative fa: fa, go back, ra, then ya and dal.
- **Joining after the alternative fa** (09:53-10:07). An alif or lam joins it from a point he shows on screen. The alif can also be placed further back. These captions are unclear.

## Lesson 8: Kaf and Lam (YouTube id IIhoMKoQTOI)

### General points
- Kaf and lam share one body. In the captions the only difference is that kaf gets a cap (the "hat") on top, and the two behave differently when connected and at the end of a word (00:04–00:12, 02:09–02:17).
- Pens. At 02:28–02:34 he changes to another pen so that all the caps fit on one line. At 03:24–03:34 he says the first cap, written at full thickness, would be far too big for this letter size, so he changes pen again. At 03:56–04:03 he says some caps use a pen half or a third as wide as the main pen, and he picks the third-width pen for cap 2.
- The lesson gives no pen angle.
- Measurements in points: the tail drops 3 points (00:36); a medial kaf rises about 4 points (10:08–10:12).
- He mentions he had not practised for two weeks, so some strokes on screen are not crisp (09:35–09:42).

### Kaf / Lam body (isolated shape, before any cap)
1. **Alif stroke** (00:12–00:20): he goes straight down, but the line is slightly curved, like an alif.
2. **Nun-like tail** (00:25–00:47): at the bottom the stroke curves back, like a nun. The drop is 3 points. The caption here reads "three points of wood", which is garbled, possibly "width". The pen goes down, touches the writing line and comes back.
   - The return can be made in several ways (00:50–01:06). In the first, the pen comes back and rises, so the counter (the space inside the letter) is drop-shaped.
3. **Thin return line** (01:08–01:16): a thin hairline goes back. He sums up the three moves as move 1 like an alif, move 2 like a nun, and move 3 the thin line going back.
4. **Head decoration, as on alif** (01:18–01:29): he goes back up and adds the same decoration as on alif. The sequence he names is down, up, back, touching in, then down. The captions only list these directions; for the exact shape, see the alif lesson.
   - The thin line sometimes runs through the decoration (01:30–01:35). In other versions the decoration is short or the alif is long; this depends on your own hand (01:38–01:48). The tail can also be flatter, so that the decoration ends before it and the two never touch (01:50–02:03). In other cases the two cross (02:05–02:09).
- If you stop here, the letter is a **lam**. For a **kaf**, add a cap on top (02:09–02:17).

### Kaf caps (six of them; "one ... six" at 06:52)
He switches pen first (02:28–02:34). He says caps are very common in this script.
- **Cap 1** (02:36–02:56)
  1. Like a ba: up, then down.
  2. Curve back and lift the pen.
  3. Add two small spirals at one place, then come back and add one spiral at another. The captions do not say exactly where.
  - He compares it with the kaf cap in Naskh (03:04), in Diwani (03:08) and in Ruq'ah, where it is very sharp (03:12). Cap 1 uses the full pen thickness (03:20–03:22). At this letter size it is too big, so he changes pen, and then the cap touches the letter (03:24–03:48). He calls it the largest kaf cap he has seen in Diwani Jali.
- **Cap 2** (03:56–04:51): uses a third-width pen. He demonstrates at full thickness and asks you to imagine the smaller pen.
  1. Go down.
  2. Go back and rotate the pen. He describes this as about two-thirds of the way (04:20–04:26).
  3. Come back. The thickness changes from thin to thick. The pen must be held very shallow, so the stroke starts thin, swells in the middle, and then comes back (04:26–04:49).
- **Cap 3** (04:52–05:05): like cap 2, but instead of going back, the pen comes back from lower down and stops on a line. The kaf body sits below it. The wording is unclear.
- **Cap 4, hamza-like** (05:07–05:33): looks like a hamza but is a kaf cap.
  1. Go up.
  2. Go back. The caption calls this a "zindani curve", which is unidentified.
  - He says this cap goes with a full-size kaf or with a connected one.
- **Cap 5, sharp** (05:35–06:12): the teacher's favourite, because he loves sharp edges (06:13–06:17).
  1. Go up and stop.
  2. Go sharply left.
  3. Go sharply up again.
  4. Come down. The caption here is garbled ("with their native shape").
  5. Finish with a shape like the alif ending.
- **Cap 6** (06:18–06:48)
  1. Go down, add one rotation, and go up.
  2. From that shape, draw a second leaf that rises cleanly out of the top.
- He says other caps exist and are all decorative; use whichever you like (06:56–07:04).

### Connections
- **Initial kaf** (07:04–07:26): write the first (alif) move and carry straight on into the next letter.
- **Kaf before alif, lam, kaf, or any tall letter** (07:23–08:16)
  1. Write a dal shape.
  2. Go up into the alif, lam or kaf.
  - He describes the combined form as the start of a dal joined to a nun-like stroke that goes down and up, with the cap placed above it (07:59–08:13). Kaf followed by lam takes the same form (08:15–08:20). He says this is the same in all scripts, and that learners find kaf+lam and kaf+alif confusing (08:22–08:32).
- **Medial kaf** (09:52–10:38)
  1. The letter before it joins in.
  2. Go down, then up.
  - The height varies, but put it at about 4 points (10:08–10:12).
  - The following letter joins at roughly the halfway point of the kaf (10:18–10:25).
- **Lam versus kaf in the middle of a word** (10:45–11:12): a lam can be raised higher, and the following letter can then join lower down, because lams drop. A kaf should join from its middle.
- **Final kaf** (11:15–11:37): same as the plain shape, except that the head decoration is removed. That decoration belongs to the lam; a final kaf has none. He shows a particular cap here. A small hamza-shaped cap can also be added, which is personal preference (11:43–11:51).
- **Should the cap touch the line?** (11:52–12:15) Sometimes. In some pieces the caps float above the whole composition, which he will show later.

### Final ba, final kaf and final lam (12:51–14:49)
- **Final ba** (12:59–13:27)
  1. Go up and stop.
  2. Come down in an even shape, so that the drop and the width are balanced.
  - He calls the result solid and comfortable to look at. The ba goes around and comes back up (13:58–14:01, 14:17–14:25).
- **Final kaf** (13:38–13:49, 14:01)
  1. Drop suddenly from the top.
  2. Stop.
  - It is more vertical than the lam.
- **Final lam** (13:49–14:49): longer than the kaf and drops further below the line (14:03–14:10). After going up, stopping and coming down, its thin line shows much more than the kaf's, and it stops below the writing line (14:27–14:41). He sums it up as going right down and very thin (14:41–14:49).
- In finished pieces these final shapes are often set very low, while the kaf caps sit high above (14:53–15:01).

### Practice words
- **kun** (kaf + nun) (08:28–09:33). The caption gives the meaning as "bee", probably a mis-hearing of "be".
  1. Kaf alif move, then down and up (08:49–08:58).
  2. Nun (09:00–09:05).
  3. A cap he says he rarely uses (09:15–09:24).
  4. The head decoration, added because the kaf is initial (09:28).
- **lan** (09:43–09:51): he says you just remove "this part" and the two are similar. This probably means that dropping the cap turns the initial kaf into an initial lam, but the remark is unclear.
- **Abu Bakr** (09:52–10:45): an example of a medial kaf.
  1. The ba joins the kaf.
  2. The kaf goes down and up, about 4 points.
  3. The ra joins at about the midpoint and sweeps down below (10:27–10:38).
  4. A cap is added (10:38–10:43).
- **kalima** (12:17–12:51): kaf+lam in the special joined shape, then lam, then mim, then ta marbuta. Finish with a cap of your choice and the dots.

### Warnings
- Memorise the cap shapes, the difference between kaf and lam, and the special kaf form before tall letters (15:12–15:19).
- Keep final kaf and final lam distinct. The kaf drops suddenly and stops, more vertical. The lam is longer and thinner and ends below the line.
- A medial kaf should join from its middle. Do not let the next letter join low, as it may on a lam.


## Lesson 9: Mim (YouTube id Lvmpy0dNRSc)

### General points
- The lesson gives no pen angle and no pen change for the mim itself.
- Measurements in points are given for body 1 and the ra-like body (see below).
- Every mim has the same head (moves 1–2). The tail ("body") comes in several variants.

### Mim head (all forms) (00:36–01:13)
1. Go up to the right (00:36–00:45).
2. Come back the same way, but let the stroke push slightly outside the head (00:45–01:07).

### Mim bodies (isolated / final)
- **Body 1** (01:13–01:49): go left, then curve down with the same curvature as ha and ain.
  - Measurements: 5 to 6 points, 3 points from the edge, and the tail sticks out about 3 points to the left of the mim (01:27–01:42). The captions do not say which dimension each number refers to.
  - He lifted the pen here only to show where the stop is. Normally this is one continuous stroke (01:42–01:49).
- **Body 2** (01:51–02:22): starts like body 1, with a smooth transition. Lower down, the stroke goes down and switches into a different shape, placed a little to the right (02:09–02:16). It can end with or without a rotation at the bottom, or be made very large (02:17–02:22).
- **Body 3, large** (02:25–03:12)
  1. Go left.
  2. Come down in an alif shape, the same as the alif's second move (02:51–02:55).
  3. At the bottom, change to half thickness for the decoration.
  4. Go up and rotate twice. He describes it as rotating back, and back again (02:58–03:08).
  - He counts bodies 1–3 as the three bodies of the mim (03:06–03:12).
- **Body 4, ra-like** (03:13–04:19): looks like a ra.
  1. Go back.
  2. Go up and down to make a bean shape (03:19–03:26).
  3. Go down 2 points (03:30–03:33).
  4. Rotate and come back.
  - During the rotation the pen turns in the hand; the caption says "receive the pen", which is garbled. This gives a smooth transition (03:37–03:43).
  - Before the circle turns, the stroke should already be thin, at half thickness (03:46–03:55).
  - The end goes back, so the tail has two curves (03:55–04:02). Clean repeat at 04:02–04:19.
- Any of these bodies can be used at the end of a word, with no special rule (04:21–04:30).

### Connections (medial and final mim) (04:33–06:24)
- A medial mim has two shapes. Which one you use depends on the letter that follows (05:00–05:17).
  - **Shape A** comes before mim, fa, ra, ya and ha, which he describes as letters joined from above (05:00–05:14). For example, in mim+ha the mim is written and the pen goes down into the ha, which can be any ha shape (05:23–05:34).
  - **Shape B** is used before all other letters. Example: mim+alif, where the mim is written and the pen goes up into the alif (05:37–05:42).
    1. Start down.
    2. Go up.
    3. Go back, hitting a particular point on the return.
    - He says the shape is two curves that you have to manoeuvre between (05:42–05:56).
  - The captions do not say which drawn body is A and which is B. That can only be inferred from the examples: before ha the stroke goes down, before alif it goes up.
- 06:00–06:18: he again shows the familiar shape and an alternative used "if it was a bear", referring to something seen in "the little heart". This is garbled, possibly "ba" and "letter ha". The meaning is unclear.
- **Final mim**: the same as a connected mim, finished with one of the bodies above (06:18–06:24).
- **Lam into mim** (10:01–10:25): write the lam and join it straight into the mim from the side, with no extra strokes. Then add the decorations. The caption calls this word "lam".

### Practice words
- **Imam** (06:31–07:28): the person who leads the prayer.
  1. Alif (06:42–06:53).
  2. Hamza (06:58).
  3. Medial mim, using the form that joins to alif (07:02–07:09).
  4. Final mim with one of the bodies. He picked the one that harmonises with the rest (07:11–07:25).
- **'ilm**, knowledge (07:30–07:59)
  1. Ain.
  2. Lam.
  3. Out into the mim with his chosen body, rotating and coming back (07:46–07:54).
  4. The ain uses the shape from its earlier lesson (07:54–07:59).
- **Muhammad** (08:01–09:58)
  1. The first mim takes the shape that goes before ha (08:33–08:41).
  2. The ha is the variant that does not go up and down (08:51–08:57).
  3. The ha joins the second mim. That mim takes the other shape, because a dal follows (08:14–08:20, 08:58–09:03).
  - Scale your letters; this part should be a bit bigger (08:44–08:51).
  - **Decorative alternative** (09:06–09:58): a full rotational ha going down, with the dal written outside it. Once harakat are added the word fills into a triangular outline. Alternatively, put a shadda (the caption says "shed") on top and decorations below.

### Warnings
- Keep the transitions smooth (02:02–02:05).
- On the ra-like body, reduce the stroke to half thickness before it turns.
- Choose the medial mim by the letter that follows.


## Lesson 10: Nun and Ha (ه) (YouTube id s9Vdh3t5028)

### General points
- **Pen angle**: held quite vertical for nun and ha (05:45–05:54). This is the only explicit pen-angle statement in these four lessons.
- The lesson gives no pen change.
- He notes that Diwani Jali varies a lot between artists. Keep an open mind and learn new forms when you see them (04:51–05:24).

### Nun, isolated, shape 1: three moves (00:13–02:21)
1. **First move** (00:20–00:51): 3 points high and 1 point wide (00:22–00:26). Go down to the right, then curve back to the left. He calls this a "wisam move", an unidentified term, perhaps from an earlier lesson. The stroke leans slightly to the left and establishes the middle line.
2. **Bowl** (00:51–01:12): go down, curve back and come up again. This move is 3 points wide, as in nun in other scripts. It is usually rounded, and he prefers it that way. Sometimes it is sharp.
3. **Finish to the midline** (01:13–01:25): the tail rises to the midline, so it ends higher.
- He mentions a 1-point drop as a way to judge how far the letter goes down (01:31–01:35). The captions do not say what it is measured from.
- **Slow repeat** (01:38–01:58)
  1. Down to the right.
  2. Curve left.
  3. Go down and rotate upward, so the bowl is circular.
  4. On the return, use the tip (corner) of the pen to draw the decorative finish at the top (01:49–01:55).
- **Dot**: in the middle, inside the bowl (01:58–02:05).
- **Variant** (02:05–02:21): the second move changes direction abruptly instead of curving. Both versions are acceptable.

### Nun, isolated, shape 2: big ba form (02:25–03:01)
- Written like a very large ba, with the same moves. Come back and place the dot. He says it is big enough to contain the small nun inside it (02:44–02:59).

### Nun, connected
- **Initial and medial**: same as ba; see the ba lesson (03:01–03:15).
- **Final**, example **min** (mim+nun) (03:17–04:24)
  1. Stop the mim at the top.
  2. Come back into a normal nun using moves 1, 2 and 3.
  - It should be neater than his first attempt (03:35). The first move goes down, then left, curves down, and rises back with the decorative top.
  - He contrasts a balanced nun with one that leans left (04:00–04:06).
- **kun** (kaf+nun) (04:24–04:51): uses the big nun built on the ba (04:30–04:44). Dot at 04:51.

### Ha (ه)
- **Initial ha** (05:27–07:06): 3 points wide (05:34–05:36), with a 1.5-point drop (05:41–05:45).
  1. Go all the way down and stop (05:54–06:01).
  2. A curve that joins the first move smoothly, then a straight run to the left (06:03–06:18). He counts it as one going down, two levelling out, three going up (06:18–06:25).
  3. Go up with a thick stroke, sticking out about 1 point beyond the letter (06:28–06:36).
  4. Come back. Watch the guide line he drew: that is where you stop and change direction (06:38–06:46).
  - These lines can also lean the other way (06:51–06:56).
  - It joins smoothly into a following ha (07:01–07:06).
- **Initial/medial ha, flatter (horizontal) version** (07:06–07:28): the form you will see most. The counter (inner space) should be eye-shaped. Then go back and repeat what the caption calls "the fa shape", which is garbled; it probably refers to the loop.
- **Final ha** (07:29–07:44): close to the final ha of Ruq'ah and Diwani. Stop, then come back down. It is small, 1 by 1 point.
- **Uncommon final ha with decoration** (07:45–08:08)
  1. Go up, then left, and stop, like an alif.
  2. Come back on the corner of the nib, sticking out slightly and cleanly from the main line.
  3. Rotate, come back, and fill in the shape.
- **Isolated ha, shape 1** (08:20–08:52): round and simple. Watch the angle of the counter.
  1. Go up and down, and stop at a set slant.
  2. Join smoothly back to the start.
  - The upper move sits a little higher than the lower one, so one space is smaller than the other (08:44–08:50). The captions do not make clear which space is meant.
- **Isolated ha, shape 2** (08:52–09:52): his favourite.
  - The starting line is fairly straight.
  1. The first move is sharp.
  2. The second move is thicker. He says it has a Thuluth feel: go down, then curve up (09:17–09:30).
  - The top looks like a cone or an egg.
  - The right side is larger than the left.
  - The counter is hazelnut-shaped (09:39–09:44).
- **Ta marbuta** takes the same isolated shapes as ha (09:52–10:05).

### Practice word
- **"ya homu"**, given as "interests him" (10:10–10:45). The captions name the letters ya, ha, mim and a final ha, so the word is likely yahummuhu.
  1. Ya joins into the ha (10:24).
  2. The ha joins a mim (10:31).
  3. The mim takes the form used before ha (10:34–10:37).
  - Watch the sharp angles and the ha's counter (10:45–10:49).

### Warnings
- Keep the nun's first move leaning slightly left, and keep its bowl round.
- On the initial ha, the thick upward stroke has to stop and turn exactly at the guide line.
- The counter of the flat initial ha should be eye-shaped.


## Lesson 11: Ya, Lam-Alif and Harakat (YouTube id cxJ2QK53rqI)

### General points
- The lesson gives no pen angle.
- **Pens**: the full-size harakat use the main pen. The small harakat use a pen about a quarter as wide (15:37–15:45). He then switches to an even smaller pen, because the quarter pen was still a bit big (16:06–16:16), and later goes back to a slightly bigger one so the marks are visible on camera (17:26–17:28). This is a quarter-width pen, not the third-width pen of lesson 8.
- **Guidelines**: for lam-alif he recommends ruling guide lines before writing (11:01–12:28). Also clean the pen whenever it gets messy (11:15–11:25).

### Ya (and alif maqsura)
- **General form** (00:43–00:51)
  1. First move down.
  2. Second move back.
  3. Then a nun-shaped bowl, as in sin, sad and nun.
- **Shape 1, short return** (00:53–02:28)
  1. Start and go straight down (00:56–01:03). This move is 3 points (00:39–00:41). He says it is about 2 points tall, and the pen thickness produces the shape (01:07–01:10).
  2. Go back, but stop short instead of reaching the other side (01:17–01:27). This makes the ya look as if it wears a hat rather than having a full-size body (01:27–01:38).
  3. The nun-shaped bowl starts from where move 2 stopped: stop, go down, come back up (01:40–01:50).
  4. The tail comes all the way back and crosses the midline (01:50–01:58). There is a 1-point gap between "those two" (01:58–02:02); the captions do not say between what.
  - Clean repeat (02:04–02:28): down, right, straight down, bowl, back up, final finish. The gap is clearly visible.
- **Shape 2, full return** (02:28–03:36)
  1. The first move starts with a slight rise of about 1 point, then goes down, about the same size (02:36–02:46).
  2. The second move runs all the way back in a smooth curve (02:46–02:56). The curves can be half a point on both sides, which unifies the shape (02:56–03:03).
  3. Finish with the nun move (03:20–03:24).
  - The gap is narrower, and the shape is more compact (03:24–03:36).
- **Style**: shape 1 looks sharp and close to Persian (Farsi) script. Shape 2 is closer to Thuluth; the caption says "phthalo". Choose to suit the piece (03:03–03:17).
- The same shape is **alif maqsura** (03:38–03:41).
- **Ya dots**: two below, spaced 1 point apart (03:41–03:47).
- **Connected**: same rules as ba. The final form looks the same; only the connection changes (03:57–04:08).
- **Nabra (tooth)**: the ya's tooth works like the ba's tooth. He says this rule holds in every script (05:21–05:41). The rest of this passage (05:41–05:59) is too garbled to use.

### Lam-alif
- **Shape 1** (06:13–08:20)
  1. **Alif move** (06:13–06:24): long, 5 points.
  2. **Lam stroke rising high** (06:26–06:51): go down like a lam. On the way up, do not stop where the strokes cross; carry on higher. That is where the stroke changes direction.
  3. **Almond loop** (06:51–07:05): one stroke goes in and another goes out, giving an almond shape. Going up to the right the stroke is thin, then slightly thick (07:07–07:12).
  4. **Return through the centre** (07:14–07:34): the next stroke comes back on the centre line. Lower down it changes direction to the left. The turn must be centred.
  5. **End** (08:12–08:18): come back with a quarter of the pen's thickness and make the finish.
  - Weight: he says there is more weight downward than upward (07:34–07:39), but then that the upper part is larger, roughly 4 points against 1.5 points for the lower part (07:53–08:06). The captions state both, so it is unclear which part is meant.
  - Distances (08:30–08:36): a gap of 1 point, and 1.5 points inside. The overall size depends on the piece; lam-alif can be large or small (08:36–08:46).
- **Shape 2: seven moves** (08:47–10:39)
  - **Directions in order** (08:53–09:10): to the right, back to the left, down-left, down-right, then back out.
  - **Numbered moves** (09:10–09:33)
    1. The alif.
    2. The second move.
    3. Out one way.
    4. Back.
    5. Down.
    6. All the way down.
    7. The ending, which is an alif ending.
  - The lower part is larger than the upper part, so the weight is at the bottom (09:33–09:45).
  - **Move 3** curves up and has two curves in it. The upper stroke closes it, and the next stroke comes back. The following stroke curves the same way (09:45–10:08).
  - In sequence (10:09–10:14): left, up, stop, back, left, right, then the final direction.
  - The shape must be centred, with two crossings (10:14–10:21).
  - **Hardest moves**: 3 and 6 (10:21–10:39).
  - **End-decoration height**: on shape 1 it drops lower. On shape 2 it stays level, because shape 2 is so large (10:47–11:00).
- **Guideline method** (11:01–12:28)
  1. Rule lines first.
  2. Make the first move.
  3. Stop the second move.
  4. Mark about 1 point on both sides, a bit more for the middle move (11:44–11:51).
  5. That middle line is the centre line for the alif part.
  6. Move 3 makes the first crossing on the centre line (11:59–12:04).
  7. Go back up. Watch the crossing on the way back, then make the final move.
  - With these lines it is easy to follow the strokes neatly.
- **Sharp versus soft**: he prefers the sharp, cone-shaped intersections. Some calligraphers write them softer; done badly, the intersection looks wobbly (12:31–12:51).
- **Shape 3, from ordinary Diwani** (12:51–13:19)
  1. A short alif.
  2. The same shape again, but it touches down at the bottom.

### Harakat and decorative marks

**Full size, written with the main pen**
- **Fatha** (13:33–13:46): curve up, run straight, curve up again, giving two curves. Full thickness.
- **Unnamed second mark** (13:48–13:58): go down, up, then back with half the pen's thickness. This is probably damma, which the recap at 15:16 calls "bom", but the captions do not name it here.
- **Madd** (13:58–14:06): a stroke down to the right, like the Arabic numeral one.
- **Leaf** (14:06–14:42): a decorative filler used to balance a piece.
  1. Start sharp and go up.
  2. Curve slightly.
  - You must rotate the pen while moving. The pen starts at a shallow angle, then turns to follow the curve (14:22–14:42).
- **Sukun** (14:42–15:09)
  1. Move up.
  2. Curve.
  3. Curve back. This curve must stick out slightly, so the last move has room and the shape looks right.
- **Tanween** (15:17–15:30): same as fatha or kasra (the caption says "fat orchestra"), doubled. Watch the distance between the two strokes.

**Small size, written with the quarter-width pen and then a smaller pen (15:34–21:59)**
The moves are the same as the full-size marks, only much smaller.
- **Fatha and tanween** (16:18–16:26).
- **Shadda** (16:27–16:35)
  1. Down, then up.
  2. Left, and stop.
  3. Back.
  - It is sharp.

## Lessons 12-14: notes common to all three

General notes for all three lessons:
- **Small pen size.** In both Lesson 12 (16:20) and Lesson 14 (20:53) the captions give the small pen as **a quarter** ("fourth") of the main pen's width, not a third. Lesson 13 (05:15) says only that the final piece uses a smaller pen. If other lessons say a third, these lessons disagree with them.
- **Stop now, finish later.** All three lessons build the piece the same way. Letters that end in a long tail or a large cup are **stopped** at a set point. Their endings are written later, once the following words or the whole line are in place. For splitting letters into strokes, that means a long ending is a separate, later stroke that starts where the letter stopped.
- **Breaks to move the hand.** In Lesson 14 (15:07) the teacher points out that some breaks are only there so he can move his hand, or because he dipped the pen (14:04). They are not part of the letterform's design.
- **Common order of work in all three lessons:**
  1. Write the words out once in Diwani Jali to see their shapes.
  2. Choose an outline and draw it lightly in pencil.
  3. Write the main letters and words to fill the outline.
  4. Add harakat with the small pen.
  5. Add mawazin (fillers): small letters such as mim, and similar shapes.
  6. Add leaves.
  7. Add the dot clusters last.
  8. Redraft by tracing.


## Lesson 12: Example 1 (YouTube `1XMfpz0fSTA`)

**Text:** the basmala, بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم. It is written in the "general shape" layout, in this case a block with one rounded end and the rest rectangular.

### Three layouts of Jali Diwani (overview, 00:00-06:27)
- **Purpose (00:09-01:10):** Jali Diwani is meant to be ornate and dense and to need decoration. The captions say it was first used for headings and "secret letters". That is probably confidential court correspondence, but the captions don't make it clear. The teacher remarks that "jali" means clear, yet the script is anything but.
- **1. Farman (01:11-03:51):** a fixed outline.
  - It curves at the right, runs fairly straight, nudges upward at the end, and has a few parts sticking out at the end.
  - Kaf caps can rise above the outline.
  - The large hollow letters can be used to break up the outline, with letters running into each other inside them. The letters named are ba/ta, jim+alif, ain+alif, the long sin, nun, kaf, and letters with a big cup.
  - The inside of the shape is left as free space, while the rest becomes nearly solid ink.
  - Mim endings can hang below the outline (02:20).
- **Waw heads and high heads alternate (02:56-03:47):**
  - Waw heads sit at one level, lower down.
  - Another pair of letters sits higher up. The captions say "the fart and the path", probably fa and qaf, but this is uncertain.
  - The heavy heads should be laid out in a regular rotation, not at random. If a waw goes in one spot, the next sits beside it, and a fa goes above.
  - Done this way, the repeated heavy heads tie the piece together, with other letters between them.
- **2. General shapes (03:51-04:27):** circle, square, drop, or "hazelnut". He gives no fixed rules except one: the negative space (the balance of inked to empty area) must be balanced.
- **3. Boat, *zawraq* (04:27-06:27):**
  - The top is flat. At the end there is a prow shape that curves around and down.
  - Underneath are "logs/pallets" (planks) made from letters. The text sits in a band at the top, and the ends of certain letters drop down from it.
  - The dropping letters are waw, ba, and possibly fa and jim. The captions are garbled here.
  - For example, a waw's tail continues all the way down, and so does a nun's. The prow is added at the end.
  - Last, add four long strokes from alif or waw that look like the poles or oars used to move a boat. He forgets the word.

### Composition method (06:28-13:42)
1. **06:46-07:16:** Write the words out first in Diwani Jali. You need both the word shapes and the outline they will fill. He had practised beforehand, and he weighed a circle against a rounded-edge shape. He picked the rounded-edge one (07:20-07:43).
2. **Bism (07:45-08:06):** ba joined to a **small sin**, then joined to the mim "that looks like a rod", which comes back on itself.
3. **Outline (08:06-08:17):** a semicircle on this side, continuing as a rectangle.
4. **Planned fillers (08:17-08:44):**

   - A second, small sin is placed below to balance the real sin.
   - A small mim could go in another gap. Decorations come later.
5. **Allah (08:44-09:46):**
   - The captions mention a "succulent" that can fill a gap. That is probably the shadda or sukun; it is unclear which.
   - The word could start in either of two places. One would make it cross the mim and hurt the mim's legibility, so he starts in the other.
   - He writes the alif of the jalala and ends it low down.
   - **Lam:** stop, go up with a short lam, not a tall one, then the ha.
   - Once the space is filled, he lowers one letter so that it touches the mim.
   - Above go the shadda and either a small upright alif or a fatha. The captions don't name the first sign clearly.
6. **al-Rahman (09:46-11:10):**
   - Another alif. Keep all the alifs alike. The lam follows, and the alif comes down.
   - **Ha-mim-nun: two forms are possible (10:13-10:29).**
     - Plain form: the ha drops into the mim, then the nun.
     - The form that fills more space, which he picks because there is a big gap between the ra and the ha. Draw the body, come back, go up, rotate into the mim, then come back with the nun. Stop.
   - The nun dot goes in. The **ha's head is added separately** after the body (11:05).
   - Shadda over the ra. A balancing mark below, to become a filler later (11:10-11:18).
7. **11:24-11:48:**

   - He could have dropped a dot and moved the alif closer to the ha, but leaves it.
8. **al-Rahim (11:51-13:04):**
   - Check that every alif **starts and stops at the same place** for consistency.
   - The ra of al-Rahim follows the ra of al-Rahman and comes down to match it.

   - Then the dots. The word ends in a curve.
9. **13:04-13:42:**

- 13:52-14:15: keep the Diwani curves, echoing curve with curve, and **rescale everything** to fit.

  - The **two ha letters look alike**, and one is brought up.
  - The nun dot goes in, he cuts back one part, and the dots of the ya go in.

### Harakat and decorations (15:57-20:21)
- **Order (16:00-16:25):**
  - Kasra first. The main important shapes come first in general.
  - Then switch to the **small pen**, given here as a quarter of the main pen.
- **16:25-17:07:**
  - Shadda.
  - Small letter signs: a ha, a mim, and a sin "for the mim". These are probably the small letter marks set under their letters, but the captions are unclear.
  - Sukun and fatha, with alternative forms of these signs available.
- **Harakat (17:03-17:10):** take care with them so they don't look odd.
- **Mawazin and leaves (17:25-17:47):** look for the **largest empty spaces** and put the filler shapes there. Keep watching the overall balance.
- **Outline revised (17:49-18:23):**

  - Something can stick up. A gap can be filled with a shape turned the other way.

- **Fillers (18:23-19:08):**
  - A small mim filler.

- **Dots come last (19:08-20:21):**

  - Leave breaks between groups. Groups never come close to each other. Place them to balance one another.
  - Where two already sit, one more is the most you should add.
  - The piece must never rely on dots for its main shape. That is why the letters have to fill it.

### Advice and rejected drafts (20:21-22:28)
- **Practice (20:31-21:09):** begin with short texts such as your name or family names. Start with three-word phrases and keep them simple.
- **Rejected circle draft (21:35-21:53):** too heavy at the top and too light at the bottom.
- **Rejected ba draft (22:00-22:10):** the sin rose after the ba to bunch everything together, and he didn't like the result.
- **Missed chance (22:10-22:28):** a shape drawn **from the mim itself** would have filled a gap and given the piece a heavy part to balance it.

### Rules stated
- Every alif starts and stops at the same height (11:51, 09:52).
- Repeated letters (two mims, two ha letters, two ra letters) should match in level and shape (12:42-12:57, 15:40).
- Avoid crossings that make a letter hard to read, such as passing through the mim (08:56-09:02).
- Letters fill the shape. Decorations only fill leftover space, and dots never form the main shape.
- Negative space must be balanced (04:17).

### Warnings
- Don't fill the composition with dots or fillers (20:05-20:13).
- Don't crowd fillers or dot groups together (18:55, 19:50).
- Watch top-heavy versus bottom-heavy balance (21:46).

### The prow (12:23-13:17)
- Come back with a curve, go up, come down, and carry on to the left, almost a circle, like the front of a boat.
- In short: up, left, then out with an ending like an alif or ra. The length is your choice.
- On the next try he goes back **thin**, then rotates, comes back, continues, and stops.

### Advice
- **23:45-24:09:** start with a shorter text with **three or four endings**, not a whole line.
- **25:46-26:53:**
  - A full draft, even a small one, shows the harakat, dots and paper size in advance, so nothing surprises you.
  - He made a small design first, then a big one.
- **Recap (27:25-28:58):**
  - Rule a top limit line.
  - Lay out the words in pencil or with a plain pen.
  - **Stop the endings and finish them only after the whole text.**
  - Then join the endings below.
  - You can swap letters for a better transition, for example using the ya of *munan* instead of the waw. The word order can be rearranged too.
  - **Bows** either reach the bottom or stop partway down the boat, but **wherever they stop they must end in a crisp, sharp ending**.

### Rules stated
- Nothing above the top line (01:49).
- Endings that make the hull and bows are stopped and finished last, in reverse order along the hull (04:37-04:48, 08:50-08:57, 13:17).
- Hull lines keep their distance from neighbouring letters, hold one angle, and end sharp (13:36-13:54).
- Bows run parallel and end together, straight down or with a small upward angle (17:44, 18:01-18:12).
- Fillers go only in the text body, never in the hull and bow area (22:51-23:11).

### Warnings
- Check early that a dropped ending won't hit a letter below (05:15-05:21, 05:45-05:49, 13:56).
- Writing over wet ink makes the pen stick (17:08).
- A stroke that is too thick won't blend with the rest. Fix it (19:19-19:25).
